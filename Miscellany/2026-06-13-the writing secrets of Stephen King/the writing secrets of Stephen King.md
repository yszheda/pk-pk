## Monsters in the Archives by Caroline Bicks review – the writing secrets of Stephen King卡罗琳·比克斯的《档案中的怪物》书评——斯蒂芬·金的写作秘密

This article is more than 2 months old

A deep dive into the horror novelist’s archives reveals pedantry, penny-pinching, and a total redraft of Carrie 深入挖掘这位恐怖小说家的档案，会发现他存在的吹毛求疵、吝啬，以及对《凯瑞》的彻底重写

When Caroline Bicks first met [Stephen King](https://www.theguardian.com/books/stephenking) she was worried. As a teenager she had scared herself silly with his books – Carrie and The Shining were the two that crept under her skin and refused to budge – but now she found herself in the odd position of being Stephen E King professor at the University of Maine. The chair had been named in honour of the English department’s most famous alumnus, and Dr Bicks was a Harvard-trained Shakespeare specialist. What, beyond a name, would they really have in common?卡罗琳·比克斯第一次见到 [斯蒂芬·金](https://www.theguardian.com/books/stephenking) 时，她很担心。青少年时期，她被他的书吓得魂飞魄散——《凯瑞》和《闪灵》是她心底深处挥之不去的两本书——但现在她发现自己处于一个奇怪的位置——成为缅因大学的斯蒂芬·E·金教授。该讲席以英语系最著名的校友命名，而比克斯博士是哈佛受训的莎士比亚专家。除了名字之外，他们还有什么共同点？

At the time of her appointment, Bicks’s employers had told her not to initiate contact with the famous author in any way. But four years into the job she got a phone call from “Steve” who turned out to be a teddy bear: “I couldn’t believe it. The man responsible for terrifying generations of readers – including me – was so … nice.” Not quite a meet-cute, but promising.在她被任命时，比克斯的雇主曾告诉她不要以任何方式与这位著名作家联系。但工作四年后，她接到了“史蒂夫”的电话，结果他是个泰迪熊：“我简直不敢相信。那个让几代读者——包括我——感到恐惧的男人，真是......不错。”虽然不算是那种可爱的邂逅，但很有希望。

This book is Bicks’s account of what happened when King gave her permission to spend a year in his archive, poring over the drafts of five of his most popular novels, including Pet Sematary, The Shining and Carrie. Bicks’s particular aim is to spot what she calls King’s “biblio‑magic” in action. She wants to identify how he chooses and places words with the intention of producing material effects on the reader’s body. How, exactly, does he make hearts beat faster, stomachs lurch and palms prickle with sweat? In On Writing, his classic how-to text of 2000, King calls what he does “telepathy in action” and Bicks wants to catch him in that act.这本书是比克斯对金允许她在档案馆待上一年时间，仔细研读他五部最受欢迎小说的草稿的叙述，包括《宠物公墓》、《闪灵》和《凯瑞》。比克斯的特别目标是发现她所称的金的“藏书魔法”的实际运作。她想识别他如何选择和放置词语，意图在读者身体上产生物质效果。他究竟是如何让心跳加速、胃部翻腾、手掌冒汗的？在他2000年的经典操作指南《论写作》中，金称自己的行为为“心灵感应的实际行动”，而比克斯想抓住他的表演。

There is no shortage of raw material for her to chew through. King’s archive is attached to the house in Bangor, Maine, which he bought with his wife, Tabitha, in 1980. Two professional archivists care for his working papers, which are catalogued and kept in a climate-controlled environment. King started writing long before the days of traceless computer editing, so the bulk of the archive consists of multiple typewritten drafts pecked out on his wife’s portable Olivetti. The huge advantage here is that these early manuscripts have gathered to themselves extra richness in the form of handwritten marginalia, in-text edits, back and forth exchanges with copy-editors, all before you get to final proofs. The result is exactly the kind of textual mulch to gladden any literary scholar’s heart.她有源源不断的原材料可供她咀嚼。金的档案收藏于缅因州班戈的这座房子上，该房子是他与妻子塔比莎于1980年购买的。两名专业档案管理员负责管理他的工作论文，这些文件被编目并在气候控制的环境中保存。金在无痕迹的电脑编辑时代之前就开始写作，因此档案的大部分是他妻子便携式Olivetti打印出来的多份打字稿。这里最大的优势在于，这些早期手稿在最终校样之前，已经积累了额外的丰富内容，包括手写边注、文内编辑、与校对员的来回沟通。结果正是那种能让任何文学学者心灵愉悦的文本覆盖物。

Bicks quickly spots what she is after in the editorial interventions on Pet Sematary, King’s novel of 1983 which many fans think is the scariest, certainly the bleakest, he ever wrote. There’s a moment early in the book where a tangle of fallen tree branches turns into a pile of moving bones. In an early draft, King writes “fingerbones clittered”, which the copy-editor circles and asks “Word OK?” King in turn replies “Word OK. A clitter is a very soft, ghostly clatter.” And there you have it. Clitter – softly insinuating – is so much scarier than a crash-bang clatter.比克斯很快在金1983年小说《宠物公墓》的编辑介入中看出了她的目标，这部小说被许多粉丝认为是他写过的最恐怖、最阴郁的作品。书的开头有一幕，一团倒下的树枝变成了一堆移动的骨头。在早期草稿中，金写下了“指骨斑驳”，校对员圈起来并问“Word OK？”金回答：“好的，没问题。“clitter”是一种非常柔软、幽灵般的碰撞声。”就是这样。Clitter——轻声暗示——比撞击声更令人害怕。

In the same manuscript, Bicks also finds the novelist resisting the copy editor’s attempts to replace the word “rattly” which King has used to describe the laboured breathing of the novel’s dying two-year-old protagonist, Gage Creed. The copy editor suggests “congested” would be better. But King knows that rattly contains within itself a whole ghastly set of subliminal associations including scavenging vermin and unquiet ghosts with their infernal chains. Congested is something a coroner would write.在同一手稿中，比克斯还发现小说家在抵抗校对编辑试图替换“rattly”一词的尝试，金用来形容小说垂死的两岁主角盖奇·克里德沉重的呼吸。校对编辑建议用“拥挤”来形容会更好。但金知道，《Rattly》本身包含了一整套令人毛骨悚然的潜意识联想，包括拾荒的害虫和带着地狱锁链的不安幽灵。“鼻塞”是验尸官会写的词。

This is the kind of close reading usually associated with academic lit crit, so it can feel odd to find it in a book aimed at King’s ardent fanbase. But Bicks deftly interweaves textual analysis with more general biographical data, gleaned from her conversations with King, both in person and via email. When she queries why the margins on the drafts of his early novels are so narrow, he explains that it was to save on the cost of paper. In the early 1970s King and his wife were broke. He was working as a high school instructor and putting in extra shifts in the launderette while Tabitha did nights at Dunkin’ Donuts. Paper was a luxury, especially at the rate King got through it (a lot ended up in the bin).这种细读通常与学术文学批评相关，因此在面向金的忠实粉丝群的书中看到它可能会让人觉得奇怪。但比克斯巧妙地将文本分析与她与金的面对面和电子邮件对话中获得的更一般的传记资料交织在一起。当她质问他早期小说草稿为何边缘如此狭窄时，他解释说是为了节省纸张成本。1970年代初，金和妻子身无分文。他当时在高中任教，还在自助洗衣店加班，而塔比莎则在Dunkin' Donuts值夜班。纸张是奢侈品，尤其是以金的快速处理速度（很多纸都被扔进了垃圾桶）。

These frugal habits lasted even once success arrived. King tells Bicks about the time that his only copy of the final draft of The Dead Zone (1979) accidentally got picked up at the airport by a woman who had mistaken his bag for hers. Only after a cross-country rescue mission was the manuscript safely retrieved. But if ever there was a case for splashing out on a photocopier, or at least some carbon paper, this was surely it.这些节俭的习惯即使成功到来后依然存在。金告诉比克斯，他唯一一份《死区》（1979）最终稿的副本在机场被一位误以为是她包的女士拿走的经历。直到一次跨国救援行动后，手稿才安全取回。但如果说有什么理由值得大手笔买复印机，或者至少买些复印纸，那这无疑是最好的选择。

King’s thriftiness endured because his 1974 breakthrough with Carrie felt like it might be a fluke. By now he had been publishing short stories for eight years and had completed three novels without success. When Carrie was finally accepted (the news came by telegram because the phone had been cut off) the Kings were able to swap their trailer for a flat. The paperback rights soon sold for $400,000, enough for King’s mother, Ruth, who had raised him singlehandedly, to leave her low-paid job. Within a year Carrie had sold a million copies, but Ruth King was dead from cancer.金的节俭之所以得以延续，是因为他1974年凭借《凯莉》的突破似乎只是偶然。此时他已经发表短篇小说八年，完成了三部小说但未获成功。当凯莉最终被录取时（消息是通过电报传来的，因为电话被切断了），国王一家成功用拖车换成了公寓。平装本版权很快以40万美元售出，足以让独自抚养金的母亲露丝辞去低薪工作。不到一年，《凯莉》销量达到一百万张，但露丝·金因癌症去世。

It is in Bicks’s close reading of Carrie that we most clearly see her interests converging with King’s. One of her academic books, Cognition and Girlhood in Shakespeare’s World, concerns the interior lives of characters such as Juliet and Ophelia, who are on the verge of puberty. Bicks argues that, far from being regarded by Shakespeare as passive or pathological, these girls’ developing adolescent brains are catalysts for some of the plays’ most vital discussions about body and soul, faith and salvation. And it is through this lens of what she calls “brainwork” that Bicks approaches Carrie, a novel that famously hinges on a schoolgirl whose first period unleashes a sudden and violent expansion of her telekinetic abilities.正是在比克斯对凯莉的细致解读中，我们最清楚地看到她的兴趣与金的兴趣相合。她的一本学术著作《莎士比亚世界中的认知与少女生活》探讨了朱丽叶和奥菲莉娅等正值青春期边缘的角色的内心世界。比克斯认为，这些女孩在莎士比亚眼中并非被动或病态，反而是推动剧中关于身体与灵魂、信仰与救赎的重要讨论的催化剂。正是通过她所谓的“脑力工作”视角，比克斯来创作《凯莉》，这部小说以一位女学生在第一次月经突然爆发心灵动力能力为主。

Of particular interest to Bicks is the change between King’s two main drafts. In an earlier version, Carrie, brutally bullied for not realising her period had arrived, finds her body becoming devilish. Horns begin to sprout on her forehead while her skull elongates so that, in the end, she resembles a monstrous lizard. Her revenge is nothing less than an Armageddon as she flies through her small-town community dispensing carnage, even managing to down a passenger plane. (King tells Bicks that he had modelled this first draft on a schlocky film from 1957 called The Brain from Planet Arous.) The second draft is closer to the finished text. Now it is Carrie’s consciousness that becomes the story’s “centre of gravity – a site of dynamic mental exchanges with the other main characters”.比克斯特别感兴趣的是金两份主要草稿之间的差异。在早期版本中，凯莉因没意识到月经来临而遭受残酷欺凌，发现自己的身体变得恶魔化。额头上开始长出角，头骨拉长，最终形似一只巨大的蜥蜴。她的复仇无异于世界末日，她飞越小镇社区，制造混乱，甚至击落了一架客机。（金告诉比克斯，他以1957年一部名为《阿鲁斯星球的大脑》的低俗电影为蓝本创作了这部初稿。）第二稿更接近成品。现在，凯莉的意识成为故事的“重心——与其他主要角色动态心灵交流的场所”。

Some of King’s “constant readers” may find themselves skipping these passages to get to the next bit of biographical revelation (his early drinking problem, say, or the fact that he thought Jack Nicholson was woefully miscast in Stanley Kubrick’s adaptation of The Shining). But for those with the patience to follow Bicks’s more erudite detours into Stephen King’s monstrosity, there is much to relish in this highly original book.金的一些“忠实读者”可能会跳过这些段落，直接进入下一段传记揭示（比如他早期的酗酒问题，或者他认为杰克·尼科尔森在斯坦利·库布里克改编的《闪灵》中选角极不合适）。但对于那些有耐心跟随比克斯对斯蒂芬·金怪物更为博学的探讨的人来说，这本极具原创性的书中仍有许多值得欣赏的地方。
