## 'You have to just draw something that you hope is funny': How Charles M Schulz created Charlie Brown and Snoopy“你必须画一些你希望自己搞笑的东西”：查尔斯·M·舒尔茨如何创造查理·布朗和史努比

**Charles M Schulz drew his beloved Peanuts strip for 50 years until his announcement on 14 December 1999 that ill health was forcing him to retire. In History looks at how an unassuming cartoonist built a billion-dollar empire out of the lives of a group of children, a dog and a bird.查尔斯·M·舒尔茨绘制了他心爱的《花生漫画》连载50年，直到1999年12月14日宣布健康状况不佳，不得不退休。《历史》讲述了一位不起眼的漫画家如何通过一群孩子、一只狗和一只鸟的生活，建立起一个价值数十亿美元的帝国。**

Charles M Schulz's timeless creation Charlie Brown may have been as popular as any character in all of literature, but the cartoonist was modest about the scope of his miniature parables. In a 1977 BBC interview, he said: "I'm talking only about the minor everyday problems in life. Leo Tolstoy dealt with the major problems of the world. I'm only dealing with why we all have the feeling that people don't like us." 查尔斯·M·舒尔茨的永恒创作的查理·布朗或许是文学中最受欢迎的角色之一，但这位漫画家对其微型寓言的范围却显得谦逊。在1977年BBC的一次采访中，他说：“我说的只是生活中那些琐碎的日常问题。列夫·托尔斯泰处理了世界上的重大问题。我只是在处理为什么我们都觉得别人不喜欢我们。”

This did not mean that he felt as if he was dealing with trivial matters. He said: "I'm always very much offended when someone asks me, 'Do I ever do satire on the social condition?' Well, I do it almost every day. And they say, 'Well, do you ever do political things?' I say, 'I do things which are more important than politics. I'm dealing with love and hate and mistrust and fear and insecurity.'" 这并不意味着他觉得自己在处理琐碎的事情。他说：“每当有人问我'我有没有讽刺社会状况？'我总是非常被冒犯。嗯，我几乎每天都这么做。他们会问，'你有没有做过政治活动？'我说，'我做的事情比政治更重要。我正在应对爱与恨、不信任、恐惧和不安全感。'”

While Charlie Brown may have been the eternal failure, the universal feelings that Schulz channelled helped make [Peanuts](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20181112-good-grief-the-beguiling-philosophy-of-peanuts) a global success. Born in 1922, Schulz drew every single Peanuts strip himself from 1950 until his death in February 2000. It was so popular that Nasa named two of the modules in its May 1969 [Apollo 10 lunar mission](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/4M9zXf4hWZhHMdhZrDhn472/seven-things-you-might-not-know-about-peanuts) after Charlie Brown and Snoopy. The strip was syndicated in more than 2,600 newspapers worldwide, and inspired films, music and countless items of merchandise. 虽然查理·布朗可能是永远的失败者，但舒尔茨所传达的普世情感帮助《 [花生漫画](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20181112-good-grief-the-beguiling-philosophy-of-peanuts) 》成为全球成功。舒尔茨生于1922年，从1950年起直到2000年2月去世，亲自绘制了每一部《花生漫画》漫画。它非常受欢迎，以至于NASA在1969年5月阿 [波罗10号登月任务](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/4M9zXf4hWZhHMdhZrDhn472/seven-things-you-might-not-know-about-peanuts) 中，以查理·布朗和史努比的名字命名了其中两个舱段。该漫画在全球2600多家报纸上连载，启发了电影、音乐和无数周边商品。

Part of its success, according to the writer Umberto Eco, was that it worked on different levels. [He wrote](http://:%20https:/www.nybooks.com/articles/1985/06/13/on-krazy-kat-and-peanuts/): "Peanuts charms both sophisticated adults and children with equal intensity, as if each reader found there something for himself, and it is always the same thing, to be enjoyed in two different keys. Peanuts is thus a little human comedy for the innocent reader and for the sophisticated." 据作家翁贝托·埃科（Umberto Eco）称，其成功部分原因是它在不同层面上发挥了作用。 [他写道](http://:%20https:/www.nybooks.com/articles/1985/06/13/on-krazy-kat-and-peanuts/) ：“《花生漫画》同样强烈地吸引成熟的成人和儿童，仿佛每个读者都为自己找到了属于自己的东西，而且总是一样的，能在两种不同的调性中享受。因此，《花生漫画》是一部适合天真读者和有品味读者的一点人性喜剧。”

Schulz's initial reason for focusing on children in the strip was strictly commercial. In 1990, he [told the BBC](https://www.facebook.com/BBCArchive/videos/139456464818589/): "I always hate to say it, but I drew little kids because this is what sold. I wanted to draw something, I didn't know what it was, but it just seemed as if whenever I drew children, these were the cartoons that editors seemed to like the best. And so, back in 1950, I mailed a batch of cartoons to New York City, to United Features Syndicate, and they said they liked them, and so ever since I've been drawing little kids." 舒尔茨最初专注于儿童漫画的原因纯粹是商业考虑。1990年，他 [对BBC说](https://www.facebook.com/BBCArchive/videos/139456464818589/) ：“我总是不愿承认，但我画小孩子是因为这才卖得出去。我想画点什么，我不知道那是什么，但每次画孩子时，这些漫画似乎是编辑们最喜欢的。所以，1950年，我把一批漫画寄到纽约市的联合特写联合出版社，他们说他们喜欢，从那以后我就开始画小孩子。”

Of Snoopy and Charlie Brown, he said: "I've always been a little bit intrigued by the fact that dogs apparently tolerate the actions of the children with whom they are playing. It's almost as if the dogs are smarter than the kids. I think also that the characters I have serve as a good outlet for any idea that I may come up with. I never think of an idea and then find that I have no way of using it. I can use any idea that I think of because I've got the right repertory company." 谈到史努比和查理·布朗，他说：“我一直对狗似乎能容忍与它们一起玩耍的孩子们的行为感到有些好奇。几乎就像狗比孩子们更聪明。我也认为我拥有的角色是表达我任何想法的好出口。我从不想到一个点子，然后发现自己根本没法用上它。我能用我想到的任何点子，因为我有合适的剧团。”

Schulz called upon some of his earliest experiences as a shy child to create the strip. As a teenager, he studied drawing by correspondence course because he was too reticent to attend art school in person. Speaking in 1977, he said: "I couldn't see myself sitting in a room where everyone else in the room could draw much better than I, and this way I was protected by drawing at home and simply mailing my drawings in and having them criticised. I wish I had a better education, but I think that my entire background made me well suited for what I do. 舒尔茨借鉴了自己作为一个害羞孩子最早的经历来创作这部漫画。青少年时期，他通过函授课程学习素描，因为他太不愿意亲自上艺术学校。他在1977年说：“我无法想象自己坐在一个房间里，房间里其他人画得比我好得多，这样我就可以通过在家画画、邮寄画作并接受批评来保护自己。我希望自己有更好的教育背景，但我认为我的整个背景让我非常适合现在的工作。

"If I could write better than I can, perhaps I would have tried to become a novelist, and I might have become a failure. If I could draw better than I can, I might have tried to become an illustrator or an artist and would have failed there, but my entire being seems to be just right for being a cartoonist." “如果我能写得比现在更好，也许我会尝试成为一名小说家，但我可能会失败。如果我画得比现在更好，我可能会尝试成为插画师或艺术家，但那会失败，但我的整个身份似乎正适合成为漫画家。”

Peanuts remained remarkably consistent despite the relentless publishing schedule, and Schulz would not let the expectations of his millions of fans become a distraction. He said: "You have to kind of bend over the drawing board, shut the world out and just draw something that you hope is funny. Cartooning is still drawing funny pictures, whether they're just silly little things or rather meaningful political cartoons, but it's still drawing something funny, and that's all you should think about at that time – keep kind of a light feeling. 尽管出版时间紧凑，《花生漫画》依然保持着惊人的稳定性，舒尔茨也不会让数百万粉丝的期待成为分心。他说：“你得弯腰去画图，屏蔽外界，只画一些你希望有趣的东西。漫画依然是画有趣的画，无论是傻乎乎的小事还是相当有意义的政治漫画，但它依然是在画一些有趣的东西，那时你只需要想着这些——保持轻松的感觉。

"I suppose when a composer is composing well, the music is coming faster than he can think of it, and when I have a good idea I can hardly get the words down fast enough. I'm afraid that they will leave me before I get them down on the paper. Sometimes my hand will literally shake with excitement as I'm drawing it because I'm having a good time. Unfortunately, this does not happen every day." “我想作曲家作曲得好时，音乐的出现比他想象的还快，而当我有了清晰的想法时，我几乎写不出足够快的词。我担心他们会在我把它们写下来之前离开我。有时候我画的时候手会因为兴奋而颤抖，因为我玩得很开心。 可惜，这种情况并非每天都会发生。”

Despite his modesty, Schulz insisted he was always confident that Peanuts would be a hit. He said: "I mean, when you sign up to play at Wimbledon, you expect to win. Obviously, there are a lot of things that I didn't anticipate, like Snoopy's going to the Moon and things like that, but I always had hopes it would become big." 尽管谦逊，舒尔茨坚称他一直相信《花生漫画》会大获成功。他说：“我的意思是，当你报名参加温布尔登时，你会期待赢球。显然，有很多事情我没预料到，比如史努比要去月球之类的，但我一直希望它会大卖。”

Schulz generally worked five weeks in advance. On 14 December 1999, fans were dismayed to learn that he would be [hanging up his pen](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/world/americas/565289.stm) because he had cancer. He said that his cartoon for 3 January 2000 would be the final daily release. It would be followed on [13 February](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/641265.stm) with the [final strip](https://www.gocomics.com/peanuts/2000/02/13) for a Sunday newspaper. [He died](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/31yHV10pQ8S4CQcSgJMN0BP/charles-schulz-a-great-and-good-man-18-february-2000) one day before that last strip ran. 舒尔茨通常提前五周工作。1999年12月14日，粉丝们失望地得知他因患癌将挂 [起笔](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/world/americas/565289.stm) 。他说他2000年1月3日的漫画将是最后一次每日发行。随后于 [2月13](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/641265.stm) 日推出了 [周日报纸的最后一条连载](https://www.gocomics.com/peanuts/2000/02/13) 。他在那条最后一期漫画播出前一天 [去世](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/31yHV10pQ8S4CQcSgJMN0BP/charles-schulz-a-great-and-good-man-18-february-2000) 了。

In it, Schulz wrote: "I have been grateful over the years for the loyalty of our editors and the wonderful support and love expressed to me by fans of the comic strip. Charlie Brown, Snoopy, Linus, Lucy... how can I ever forget them..." 舒尔茨在信中写道：“多年来，我一直感激编辑们的忠诚，以及漫画连载粉丝们对我表达的热情和爱。查理·布朗，史努比，莱纳斯，露西......我怎么可能忘记他们......”

Back in 1977, Schulz insisted that the cartoonist's role was mostly to point out problems rather than trying to solve them, but there was one lesson that people could take from his work. He said: "I suppose one of the solutions is, as Charlie Brown, just to keep on trying. He never gives up. And if anybody should give up, he should." 早在1977年，舒尔茨就坚持认为漫画家的角色主要是指出问题，而不是试图解决问题，但人们可以从他的作品中吸取一个教训。他说：“我想解决方案之一就是，像查理·布朗一样，继续努力。他从不放弃。如果有人应该放弃，那就是他应该放弃。”
