## The Man Who Reads Books For a Living (One Every Two Days)靠读书为生的人（两天读一本）

## You Have Clarke Speicher to Thank (Or Blame) For the 你要感谢（或归咎于）克拉克·斯皮歇尔促成了Recent Train Dreams Adaptation 近期的《火车梦》改编作品

When Clarke Speicher (spike-er) asked how I liked the screen adaptation of *Train Dreams*, Denis Johnson’s novella following the solitary logger Robert Granier in the early 20th-century American West, he was actually asking whether it measured up to its source material. That is, after all, the question about adaptations. Still, it felt loaded. If it had been anyone else, I would’ve felt at liberty to prattle without worrying whether I’d arrived at any kind of thesis. That I love the book was beside the point. I felt caught out because it was Clarke doing the asking. But he isn’t an author, screenwriter, director, producer, critic, agent, or editor. He isn’t a journalist or influencer.当克拉克·斯派尔（发音为斯派克-尔）问我如何评价《火车梦》的银幕改编版时——这部改编自丹尼斯·约翰逊的中篇小说，讲述的是20世纪初美国西部孤独伐木工罗伯特·格拉尼耶的故事——他实际上是在问这部改编作品是否比得上原著。毕竟，这也是所有改编作品都会面临的问题。不过，这个问题却让人感觉别有深意。如果是别人问起，我本可以随意畅谈，不用担心自己是否得出了什么定论。我是否喜欢这本书根本无关紧要。我之所以感到局促不安，是因为发问的人是克拉克。但他既不是作家、编剧、导演、制片人、评论家、经纪人，也不是编辑，甚至不是记者或网红博主。

Clarke is something much more specific and much rarer: a professional book reader who evaluates literature specifically for screen adaptation. So after a few seconds of mealy-mouthed equivocation about *Train Dreams*, I came to my senses and flipped the question back on him. A few drinks later, we were talking about his profession, how it works, and what adaptation really means.克拉克的身份要具体且罕见得多：他是一名专业的书籍阅读者，专门评估文学作品以进行影视改编。所以在对《火车梦》支支吾吾地含糊其辞了几秒后，我回过神来，把问题反问给了他。几杯酒下肚后，我们聊起了他的职业、工作方式，以及改编的真正含义。

In his mid-40s and unassuming, Clarke is the rare interlocutor who seems to listen without waiting to speak, a far cry from people in the biz whose stock-in-trade is summed up by the very word: production. He has glasses, a solid build, a short gray beard. His thoughts tend to outpace his ability to articulate them in a first pass. He smiles a lot. There is something gentle and teddy-bear-ish about him, but it’s tempered by a New Yorker’s world-weariness. I’ve known him a long time, spent many nights talking (and drinking) with him into the small hours about books, movies, love, dreams, life. “The best adaptations take the basic idea and transform it into cinematic terms,” he says. “Which I’m realizing does sound very dumb saying it out loud,” he chuckles at himself.克拉克四十多岁，为人谦逊，是少有的倾听者——他会认真聆听，而非急着接话，这与影视圈的人截然不同，后者的核心本事一言以蔽蔽之就是“制作”。他戴着眼镜，身材结实，留着短短的灰胡子。他的思绪往往快过初次表达时的措辞能力。他常常微笑，身上透着温和憨厚的气质，却又被纽约人特有的世故冲淡了几分。我认识他很久，曾无数个深夜与他畅谈（也畅饮），从书籍、电影到爱情、梦想、人生，聊到凌晨。“最优秀的改编会抓住核心创意，将其转化为电影语言，”他说，“我现在才发现，这么说听着真挺傻的。”他自嘲地笑了笑。

An executive might walk into a meeting with an author, producer, potential directors, stars, prepared to discuss potential multi-million dollar deals armed only with Clarke’s synopsis.一位高管可能只拿着克拉克的剧情梗概，就走进与编剧、制片人、潜在导演和明星的会议，准备商谈可能高达数百万美元的交易。

His work forces a near-daily conversation between two halves of Clarke’s brain: the lifelong reader who devours books and the cinephile who dreams in celluloid. The nuts and bolts are this: an agent, executive, or producer sends Clarke a book or manuscript, blind. He reads it and writes a detailed beat-by-beat synopsis of the core scenes, settings, conflicts, and characters. Here, he quotes essential dialogue and, if any sentence-level writing stands out, includes excerpts. He then steps back to evaluate how the book might actually function on screen, that is, which elements are inherently cinematic, what can and can’t be rendered visually or dramatized, and what kind of movie or series the material wants to be. This is where the tension stretches tightest.他的工作几乎每天都要让克拉克大脑的两个部分展开对话：一个是嗜书如命的终身读者，另一个是沉浸在胶片光影中的电影爱好者。具体流程是这样的：一位经纪人、高管或制片人会匿名给克拉克寄去一本书或一份手稿。他读完后，会撰写一份详细的逐幕梗概，涵盖核心场景、背景设定、冲突和人物。在这份梗概中，他会引用关键对白，若有某句文字写得格外出彩，也会摘录下来。随后，他会进一步分析这本书改编成电影的实际可能性，也就是哪些元素天生具有电影感，哪些内容可以或无法通过视觉或戏剧化方式呈现，以及这些素材最终会成为一部怎样的电影或剧集。这也是整个工作中最考验功力的环节。

Clarke may love a book but, considering aesthetics, a conceptual budget, the likely target audience, possible star power attached, how similar projects have fared, and basic feasibility, his affection may only have so much bearing on the binary at which he must, finally, land: a recommendation to either pass and go no further, or consider and let it build momentum.克拉克或许真心喜爱一本书，但综合考量美学风格、概念预算、潜在目标受众、可能涉及的明星影响力、类似项目的过往表现以及基本可行性后，他的这份喜爱对他最终必须做出的二选一抉择可能影响有限：要么否决项目、就此打住，要么予以考虑、让其逐步推进。

He calls this report “coverage.” It serves as reference for people in rarified air Clarke has never been invited to breathe. But he harbors no ambition to scale Hollywood heights. He is, if the arc of his career is any indication, happy to be something of a brain trust. “Not every executive can read every book,” he explains. “Everybody’s busy and they don’t have time to read everything.” An executive might walk into a meeting with an author, producer, potential directors, stars, prepared to discuss potential multi-million dollar deals armed only with Clarke’s synopsis. The pressure he feels comes from real stakes.他将这份报告称作“内容概览”。这份报告是为那些身处精英圈层、克拉克从未有机会涉足的人提供的参考。但他并无进军好莱坞巅峰的野心。从他的职业轨迹来看，他很乐意成为一名智囊。“并非每位高管都能读完每一本书，”他解释道，“大家都很忙，没有时间通读所有内容。”一位高管或许只需拿着克拉克的内容提要，就能在与作者、制片人、潜在导演和明星的会面中，准备好洽谈数百万美元的潜在合作。他所承受的压力，源于实实在在的利害关系。

Still, he’s careful not to overstate things. “I don’t want to misconstrue how important I am to this process,” he says. “I actually don’t know.” He gives a good-natured shrug. His involvement begins and ends with his coverage. “I write this report and I email it and it goes into the ether,” he says. “I don’t know if anybody reads it or not… Then someone will tell me ‘your comments were really important to this executive. It was really helpful in explaining why this story works.’” 不过他很谨慎，不会把话说得太满。“我不想夸大自己在这个过程中的重要性，”他说，“事实上，我也不清楚。”他友善地耸了耸肩。他的参与仅限于自己负责的报道部分。“我写完这篇报道，发完邮件，它就进入了网络空间，”他说，“我不知道有没有人看……之后会有人告诉我‘你的评论对这位高管真的很重要。这对解释为什么这篇报道有价值帮了大忙。’”

“I never imagined reading books and turning them into movies was a job.” “我从未想过把书改编成电影也是一份工作。”

Growing up in Delaware, Clarke imagined a different career. He wanted to be an entertainment journalist, to review movies. “I thought I could do that for my local newspaper or something.” He tells me his highest aspiration was to write for *Entertainment Weekly*. “I never imagined reading books and turning them into movies was a job. I never really knew anything about that kind of job existing before.” In 2001, 20-year-old English major Clarke was reporting on a local film festival for his college newspaper. He met a film executive who offered him an internship. Clarke jumped.克拉克在特拉华州长大时，曾设想过另一种职业。他想成为一名娱乐记者，影评人。“我以为我可以在当地报社之类的地方做这份工作。”他告诉我，他最大的志向是为《娱乐周刊》撰稿。“我从没想过把书改编成电影也是一份工作。在此之前，我压根不知道还有这种职业。”2001年，20岁的英语专业学生克拉克正在为校报报道当地的电影节。他遇到了一位电影高管，对方给了他一个实习机会。克拉克立刻抓住了这个机会。

By 2002 he was in New York, reading scripts for one of the big indie studios. Part of every production assistant’s job is to help their boss thin the screenplay slush pile. But “because I can read books fast, I started doing that,” Clarke says. By his own calculation, he could read a hundred pages an hour. “I was always a big reader,” he says. “I loved books but movies are the thing I loved more.” Reading books also paid better than scripts. “At that time it was just a bunch of bad horror novels. Dean Koontz, that kind of thing.” It turned out that speed, a cinephile’s fluency, and an anxious temperament that prohibits laziness made him more valuable than your run-of-the-mill PA. “It just happened that I was suited to it.” 到2002年，他身处纽约，为一家大型独立电影工作室审阅剧本。每个制片助理的工作之一都是帮上司筛选堆积如山的未投稿剧本。但“因为我看书速度快，我就开始做这份工作”，克拉克说。据他自己估算，他一小时能读一百页。“我一直很爱读书，”他说，“我喜欢书，但电影是我更热爱的东西。”读书的报酬也比写剧本高。“当时那些书无非都是些糟糕的恐怖小说，像迪恩·孔茨这类作家的作品。”事实证明，阅读速度、影迷的鉴赏力，以及让他无法懈怠的急躁性格，都让他比普通的制片助理更有价值。“只是恰好我适合这份工作罢了。”

Smash cut to 25 years later. 镜头猛地切换到25年后。

Clarke is a freelancer staying afloat in the gig economy. He still reads potboilers, YA novels, and the like but capital-L literature remains his wheelhouse. Name the season’s hot novel or some totemic masterwork and he’s probably read it. “Sometimes I’ll get sent a new book by a major author,” he says, adding that it is not unusual to receive a first-draft Word document, typos and all. “I’m probably one of the first people who’s gotten to read \[a new major book\], and that’s exciting to me.” Solicitations can come from anywhere, including newer power players like Apple and Netflix. “Somebody will contact me who said that I was recommended by somebody I have not spoken to in ten years,” he says. The implication is clear: this is a specific problem to which Clarke is a reliable solution. “That’s part of the reason why I still have a Yahoo email address. Because that’s what I’ve had this whole time.” 克拉克是一名在零工经济中勉强维持生计的自由职业者。他依然读低俗小说、青少年小说之类的作品，但纯文学始终是他的核心专长。说出当季的热门小说或某部标志性经典作品，他大概率都读过。“有时我会收到一位知名作家寄来的新书，”他说，还补充说收到一份包含所有排版错误的初稿Word文档也并不稀奇。“我可能是第一批读到\[某部新知名作品\]的人之一，这对我来说很有吸引力。”约稿邀约可能来自任何地方，包括苹果、网飞这类新兴巨头。“有人联系我，说我是一位十年没联系过的人推荐的，”他说。言下之意很明显：这是一个特定的需求，而克拉克是可靠的解决方案。“这也是我还在用雅虎邮箱的原因之一。因为我一直用的就是这个邮箱。”

To make his nut, Clarke completes about six books a week. He mentions, offhandedly, that he has read “literally thousands of books,” so I do the rough math: even allowing for time off, that works out to roughly 300 books a year, or well over 6,000 across two decades. And that is just the professional tally. One summer a few years ago, for pleasure, he read all of *In Search of Lost Time*.为了达成目标，克拉克每周大约完成六本书的阅读。他随口提到自己“确实读了数千本书”，于是我粗略算了一下：即便算上休息时间，这也相当于每年约300本，二十多年下来早就超过6000本了。而这还只是工作层面的阅读量。几年前的一个夏天，为了消遣，他读完了《追忆似水年华》的全部内容。

Clarke is about as close to a Hollywood insider as someone who never dips a toe into production gets. His name doesn’t appear in credits, but he still feels pride when something that he championed winds up in theaters.克拉克算得上是从未涉足影视制作的人里最接近好莱坞核心圈层的一位了。他的名字从未出现在演职员表中，但当自己力挺的作品最终登上院线时，他依然会感到由衷的自豪。

Books will remain books, and movies based on books will keep getting made, but the pipeline between publishing and film has changed almost beyond recognition.书籍依旧是书籍，改编自书籍的电影也会不断被制作出来，但出版与电影之间的衔接流程已经发生了翻天覆地的变化。

For years, he was the principal reader for a certain EGOT-winning, since-disgraced mega-producer who, it was rumored, kept a cache of cell phones in his desk because he so often threw and broke them. At a social function, someone who worked in development for this producer told Clarke that he was “secretly one of the most powerful men in Hollywood because \[this producer’s\] opinion mattered the most and \[Clarke\] was one that was giving him the opinion.” Production partners would approach producer-X enthusiastic about a book, and the producer would ask, “‘But what did Clarke think about it?’ If I didn’t like something, often neither did he. And if he didn’t like something it could be dead in the water. That was why I was, secretly, very powerful and didn’t know it despite being a person who could barely pay his credit card bills.” 多年来，他一直是某位曾获EGOT大奖、后来身败名裂的顶级制片人的主要审稿人。有传言说，这位制片人在办公桌里藏了一批手机，因为他总把手机扔出去摔坏。在一次社交场合，这位制片人的开发部门工作人员对克拉克说，他“其实是好莱坞最有权势的人之一，因为\[这位制片人的\]意见至关重要，而\[克拉克\]是能给他提供核心意见的人。”制片合作方会兴致勃勃地拿着一本书去找X制片人，而制片人总会问：“‘那克拉克觉得这本书怎么样？’如果我不喜欢某样东西，他通常也不喜欢。而要是他不喜欢，那这事可能就泡汤了。这就是为什么我其实手握重权，却对此一无所知，尽管我连信用卡账单都快还不起了。”

Surviving two and a half decades in any industry means being nimble when the ground inevitably shifts. Books will remain books, and movies based on books will keep getting made, but the pipeline between publishing and film has changed almost beyond recognition during Clarke’s tenure. “It used to be that every production company or film studio had a New York office specifically to work with the publishing industry,” he says. Those days are gone. “When I first started I had to go into an office and pick up a physical copy of a book or a manuscript.” Now everything arrives by email, and he hasn’t set foot in an office in about seven years. “I know that every company has somebody who does \[this same work\],” he says. “I guess a lot of people who do this are based in LA, maybe.” 在任何行业中存活二十五年，都意味着在局势不可避免地发生变动时保持灵活应变。书籍依旧是书籍，改编自书籍的电影也会持续被制作出来，但在克拉克任职期间，出版与影视之间的对接流程已经发生了翻天覆地的变化。“过去每家制片公司或电影制片厂都设有纽约办事处，专门对接出版行业，”他说。这样的日子已经一去不复返了。“我刚入行时，还得亲自去办公室取纸质版的书籍或手稿。”如今所有资料都通过邮件发送，他已经大约七年没踏足过办公室了。“我知道每家公司都有做\[这份工作\]的人，”他说，“我想很多做这行的人大概都在洛杉矶吧。”

I ask whether that makes it harder to compare notes with contemporaries. He smiles and very matter-of-factly tells me that, a few years ago, at a brunch hosted by a major production company, “There were two other readers there. That’s the first time I ever met anybody in person.” Shocked, I make him repeat himself, and again he says: “I’ve never met anybody else who does this.” 我问这是否让你更难和同行交流心得。他笑了笑，非常平静地告诉我，几年前在一家大型影视公司举办的早午餐会上，“当时还有另外两位读者在场。那是我第一次见到真人同行。”我很震惊，让他再说一遍，他又说：“我从没见过其他做这行的人。”

We all have a book or two whose unmade movie plays in our mind at IMAX scale. Every scene is shot on location, the soundtrack is wall-to-wall bangers, and the cast is perfect with a few key roles filled by actors in their bygone prime and maybe one or two from beyond the grave. But, within the constraints of reality, what would actually make that vision work? Where would you even begin?我们每个人心里都有一两本书，它们未被拍成的电影会在脑海里以IMAX巨幕的规模上演。每一个场景都实地取景，电影原声带首首都是爆款，演员阵容堪称完美——有几位关键角色由处于演艺黄金期的演员饰演，或许还有一两位是已故的演员。但在现实的限制下，究竟是什么能让这个构想落地呢？你又该从何着手呢？

“If you can’t summarize the basic idea of a book in a sentence, it’s probably gonna be harder to adapt.” “如果你一句话都概括不了一本书的核心思想，那要把它改编成作品可能会更难。”

“The kernel—the idea. That’s the first thing that grabs me,” Clarke says. “What’s different and interesting. Is it the setting, the setup?” What he calls a book’s “hook” is the aspect of its central conceit with enough gravity to pull the reader in. He gives Karen Russell’s recent novel *The Antidote* as an example. “I thought that was very compelling, a really original idea. It’s a detective novel about, like, psychics during the dustbowl. That’s really captivating.” “核心——那个创意。这是最先吸引我的东西，”克拉克说，“有什么与众不同、有趣的地方。是故事背景，还是开篇设定？”他所说的一本书的“钩子”，是其核心创意中足够有吸引力、能将读者牢牢吸引的部分。他以卡伦·拉塞尔的新作《解药》为例。“我觉得那本书非常有吸引力，创意十分新颖。这是一部讲述大萧条时期通灵侦探的侦探小说，真的很抓人。”

Another, more pointed example is Hanya Yanagihara’s *A Little Life*. Clarke says that book “is just a series of terrible things happening to a person… I don’t want to see a movie about that.” Whereas, “ *Hamnet* is a story about grief and something terrible happening to a person but it has the hook: it’s Shakespeare.” And that hook, he says, can’t be added on—it has to be baked-in. “If you can’t summarize the basic idea of a book in a sentence, it’s probably gonna be harder to adapt.” 另一个更尖锐的例子是叶山洋理子的《小生活》。克拉克表示，这本书“只是一系列糟糕的事情发生在一个人身上……我不想看关于这个的电影。”而《哈姆内特》“是一个关于悲伤和一个人遭遇不幸的故事，但它有个吸引人的点：它是莎士比亚的作品。”他说，这个吸引人的点不是后期加上去的——必须是与生俱来的。“如果你不能用一句话概括一本书的核心思想，那么改编它可能会更难。”

Within that larger framework, Clarke looks for characters who “are interesting or unique—sympathetic in some interesting way or have unique attributes or a point of view.” But because film is visual, characters cannot merely feel, react, or reflect on the page. They need to do things that push the story along. Clarke puts it plainly: “It’s about \[the potential adaptation\] being a visual medium.” And characters who serve that medium “aren’t just interesting but doing dynamic things, not just brooding. That could be interesting to read but is harder to make a movie about.” 在这个更大的框架下，克拉克寻找的是那些“有趣或独特的角色——以某种有意思的方式富有同情心，或是拥有独特的特质与观点”。但由于电影是视觉媒介，角色不能仅在文字层面产生情感、做出反应或进行思考。他们需要做出推动剧情发展的行为。克拉克直白地表示：“关键在于\[这部潜在改编作品\]作为一种视觉媒介的属性。”而适配这一媒介的角色“不仅要有趣，还要做出富有动态的举动，而非一味地沉思。这种角色读起来或许很有意思，但要改编成电影却难度更大。”

Outside the demands of his job, Clarke, like the rest of us, can appreciate a piece of writing without having to justify it. Professionally, he cannot. “It sounds stupid but, when you’re adapting something you’re adapting its plot, not necessarily the writing.” That distinction matters. The poeticism and stylistic flourish that give a novel heft and make it singular may also make it harder to translate to the screen. “A book that has wonderful prose and really gives you a great sense of a character in the world and what they’re doing, but it’s all thoughts—that would make it more difficult to make a great movie.” 抛开工作要求，克拉克和我们其他人一样，能欣赏一篇文字而无需为其辩解。但在工作中，他却不能这么做。“这听上去很傻，但当你改编某部作品时，你改编的是它的情节，未必是文字本身。”这种区别至关重要。赋予小说厚重感与独特性的诗意和华丽文风，也可能让它更难被搬上银幕。“一本书文字优美，能让你真切感受到角色身处的世界以及他们的所作所为，但通篇都是内心独白——那会让它很难拍成一部出色的电影。”

He cites *Mrs. Dalloway*. “That was adapted into a movie that nobody really remembers. And Vanessa Redgrave was in that!” He points out that award-winning films are very rarely based on award-winning books. “Honestly, it’s easier to adapt a thriller than some high literary thing.” That is not a judgment of merit. Some of the most beloved films were drawn from books unlikely to appear on a college literature syllabus. *Die Hard, Vertigo, The Exorcist*. “More people have seen those movies than have read the books, I’d say,” Clarke says and speculates that “a lot of people probably don’t even know that *Silence of the Lambs* is based on a book. Or *The Godfather* for that matter.” Still, Clarke’s love of literature and affinity for great prose can assert themselves now and then. “There’s been times when a book reaches a point and maybe I’m not that into it,” he says. “Then something happens that makes me reconsider the whole thing. A sequence that’s so beautiful and amazingly written that maybe \[the adaptation\] is worth doing.” 他提到了《达洛维夫人》。“那本书被改编成了电影，没什么人真的记得。而瓦妮莎·雷德格雷夫还出演了那部电影呢！”他指出，获奖电影很少改编自获奖书籍。“说实话，改编惊悚片比改编一些纯文学作品要容易得多。”这并非对作品优劣的评判。一些最受喜爱的电影，其原著都不太可能出现在大学文学课程的教学大纲里，比如《虎胆龙威》《迷魂记》《驱魔人》。“我敢说，看过这些电影的人比读过原著的人多得多，”克拉克说，他还猜测，“很多人可能都不知道《沉默的羔羊》是改编自小说，或者说《教父》也是。”不过，克拉克对文学的热爱和对优美文笔的偏爱，时不时还是会流露出来。“有时候，读一本书读到某个阶段，我可能没那么感兴趣了，”他说，“但随后发生的一些事会让我重新审视这本书。如果书中有某个情节写得无比优美、惊艳，那这部作品的改编或许就值得去做。”

The goal is an adaptation that stands on its own. If strict fidelity gets in the way, it can be treated as immaterial.目标是做出一个能独立立足的改编版本。如果严格忠于原作会成为阻碍，那么这种忠实就可以被视为无关紧要的因素。

When Clarke recommends a book for further consideration, he is usually recommending one of two things: either the underlying idea can serve as a foundation for something larger, or the book is adaptable more or less as-is. One route involves interpolation, recontextualization, and refocusing; the other approaches transposition. *West Side Story* and Baz Luhrmann’s *Romeo + Juliet*, respectively. For audiences, fidelity is often the real sticking point. But pressure to pay that kind of fan-service seldom makes an adaptation better. “I hate to invoke *Harry Potter*,” Clarke says. “But the first two films were very faithful. The third does its own thing and many people think that’s the best one.” Alex Garland’s *Annihilation* based on Jeff VanderMeer’s beloved sci-fi series, for example. “That’s one of those movies that I think a lot of people who didn’t read the book liked because it was so weird.” Indeed: Critics tended to react positively to the film, whereas it was almost universally panned by fans of the series. Clarke liked both the film and books, but he gets it: “if you had read the book and you were expecting \[its unique imagery realized onscreen\] then you didn’t get it, yeah, you would be disappointed.” 当克拉克推荐一本书供进一步考量时，他通常是在推荐两种情况之一：要么其核心理念可作为更宏大事物的基础，要么这本书基本可以直接改编。一条路径涉及增补内容、重新设定语境和调整重点；另一条路径则是改编移植，分别对应《西区故事》和巴兹·鲁赫曼的《罗密欧+朱丽叶》。对观众而言，忠于原著往往是真正的关键难点。但为迎合粉丝喜好而施加的这种压力，很少能让改编作品变得更好。“我不想提《哈利·波特》，”克拉克说，“但前两部电影都非常忠于原著。第三部则有自己的风格，很多人认为这是最好的一部。”比如亚历克斯·加兰的《湮灭》改编自杰夫·范德米尔备受喜爱的科幻系列小说。“这部电影是那种很多没读过原著的人都喜欢的作品，因为它太独特了。”事实也确实如此：影评人对这部电影的评价大多积极，而它却几乎遭到了该系列粉丝的一致差评。克拉克既喜欢电影也喜欢原著，但他也能理解：“如果你读过原著，期待在银幕上看到\[书中独特的画面呈现\]，结果却没看到，那你肯定会失望。”

As far as Clarke’s work goes, each book has to be treated on its own terms. The goal is an adaptation that stands on its own. If strict fidelity gets in the way, it can be treated as immaterial. “Sometimes a book has a really good central hook to it and \[a successful adaptation would be\] expanding upon that rather than going deep into the weeds of all the details of the story… sometimes the best adaptations are the ones that take the most liberties.” 就克拉克的作品而言，每本书都得单独看待。创作改编作品的目标是让其能独立立足。如果严格忠于原著会阻碍这一点，那么这种忠实就可以被视为无关紧要。“有时候一本书有一个非常好的核心亮点，而成功的改编应该是围绕这个亮点进行拓展，而非深入钻研故事的所有细枝末节……有时，改编得最不拘一格的作品反而是最出色的。”

Years ago, he was sent a rambling Thomas Pynchon novel. This was just after P.T. Anderson’s adaptation of *Inherent Vice* landed with a thud. In his report, Clarke argued that while the plot of this other Pynchon book could technically work without all its “strangeness and digression,” removing those elements would also remove what made the book “an amazing postmodern novel.” He concluded that the book, as written, posed “significant challenges for development.” He tells me that this is one of the rare times when he loved the book but just couldn’t see the adaptation working. He recommended a pass. The book was *Vineland*. And *One Battle After Another*, was his favorite movie of 2025. “I wasn’t even sure it was considered an adaptation until I saw the credits.” 几年前，有人送了他一本情节杂乱的托马斯·品钦小说。那正是保罗·托马斯·安德森改编的《性本恶》反响平平之后不久。克拉克在报告中指出，尽管这本品钦另一部小说的情节在技术层面上可以剥离所有“怪异与离题之处”，但去掉这些元素，也会抹去这本书之所以成为“一部了不起的后现代小说”的核心特质。他总结称，按原著呈现的内容给改编工作带来了“重大挑战”。他告诉我，这是他为数不多的既深爱原著、却又看不出改编能成功的情况之一，于是建议放弃改编。这本书就是《葡萄园》。而《一场接一场的战斗》则是他2025年最爱的电影。“看到片尾字幕之前，我甚至不确定它会被视作一部改编作品。”

The limited series, Clarke says, is where hi-fi book adaptations may have their best chance of flourishing. “It’s great, especially for books… Something like *The Corrections* or *The Amazing Adventures of Kavalier and Clay—* these were books that they spent years trying to turn into a movie and they couldn’t do it… now it would be limited series. You couldn’t easily turn these 550-page books that cover years or decades into a movie.” 克拉克表示，限定剧是高保真书籍改编作品最有可能大放异彩的形式。“这很棒，尤其是对于书籍而言……像《纠正》或《卡瓦利埃与克莱的奇妙冒险》这类作品，人们曾花多年时间试图将其改编成电影，但都未能成功……如今它们会被拍成限定剧。你很难把这些篇幅长达550页、跨越数年甚至数十年的书籍改编成一部电影。”

Sometimes he’s sent stone-cold classics. Those are their own unique problems. “ *Silas Marner* or something—what am I supposed to say? Shakespeare? Am I supposed to say, ‘no, it’s gonna be difficult to adapt *A Midsummer Night’s Dream?*’ It’s hard to know.” In those cases or others wherein the book in question has obvious value but doesn’t map cleanly onto standard dramatic architecture, Clarke may imagine an arthouse film, something artistically serious but commercially uncertain. “Even if there isn’t a ton of plot getting from A to B and \[a book is\] more about an experience of life or something, you usually get that through performances and cinematography.” He might give a film like that a “consider” if the project could be helmed by “a visionary director. Somebody who can turn it into something visually interesting but has a real moving plot.” His touchstone is often Terrence Malick, whose tone-poem style has been imitated so often (*Train Dreams* included) that it nearly forms a genre of its own. But, he says, “I’ve been told ‘stop referring to Malick, you’re scaring people away!’” 有时他会收到一些堪称经典的冷硬之作。这类作品也有其独特的难题。“比如《织工马南》之类的书——我该说什么好？是说莎士比亚吗？还是该说‘不行，《仲夏夜之梦》很难改编’？实在拿不准。”遇到这类情况，或是其他书籍本身价值显著，却无法完美契合标准戏剧结构的情形，克拉克可能会设想一部艺术院线电影，一种艺术层面严肃、但商业前景不明朗的形式。“即便一本书没有大量推动情节从A发展到B，更多是关乎人生体验之类的内容，你通常也能通过表演和镜头语言传达出来。”如果项目能由“一位有远见的导演执导，他既能把作品拍成视觉上极具看点的影片，又能保留扎实的剧情主线”，他或许会给这类电影一个“考虑”的机会。他常以特伦斯·马利克为标杆，后者那种诗意叙事的风格被模仿了无数次（包括《 Train Dreams》），几乎自成一派。但他说：“有人跟我说‘别再提马利克了，你这么说会把人吓跑的！’”

When I call Clarke a writer, he demurs. Some of his coverage runs 12, 16, 20 pages. “I’ve had to write a hundred-page synopsis of a book before,” he tells me. I ask if he thinks he’s developed a voice as a writer. He seems apprehensive, and I get the sense that the insinuation that he’s a writer trips him up. But I may be imagining that. “Sometimes I do think about it, whether or not \[my writing has\] a clear personality,” he says. “Especially nowadays I feel more pressure to be detailed in a way that AI can’t summarize, trying to capture more of the atmosphere and mood than a straightforward Wikipedia summary. I try to capture tone and have quotes and dialogue so \[the clients\] have a better sense of how the story flows.” The comparison he reaches for is not an author, but tall-tale legend John Henry who, competing against a machine, won, then died from exhaustion—all so a corporation could realize its industrial project.我称克拉克是作家时，他却表示否认。他的一些报道篇幅长达12页、16页甚至20页。“我也曾写过一本长达百页的书籍梗概，”他告诉我。我问他是否认为自己已经形成了作为作家的个人风格。他显得有些局促不安，我能感觉到，“作家”这个暗示让他有些不知所措。不过这或许是我的错觉。“我有时确实会想，\[我的文字\]是否有鲜明的个人风格，”他说，“尤其是现在，我更想把内容写得细致入微，做到AI无法概括的程度，努力捕捉更多的氛围与情绪，而非简单的维基百科式概述。我会注重语气的把握，加入引语和对话，让\[客户\]能更好地理解故事的脉络。”他用来作比的不是哪位作家，而是传奇人物约翰·亨利——这位人物与机器对决并取胜，最终却力竭而亡——而这一切，只是为了让一家企业能实现其工业项目。

“I usually have two days to read a book. Sometimes more. Most people I work for now are relaxed.” “我通常有两天时间读一本书。有时候会更久。现在我服务的大多数客户都很通情达理。”

Clarke and I met more than a decade ago, when I was a bartender and he was a regular. On learning what he did, I felt a stab of envy. Who wouldn’t want to read and write about books all day? Since getting to know Clarke and his work better, I have revised that feeling. “There have been times when I’m working from eight in the morning until eleven o’clock at night or one o’clock in the morning,“ he says. Last year, he says, there was a stretch when “it was just a nightmare… Every night I was working until 11 and I don’t know why.” 克拉克和我相识十多年了，那时候我是一名调酒师，他是常客。得知他的职业后，我心里一阵羡慕。谁不想整天都在读写书籍呢？但在进一步了解克拉克和他的工作后，我改变了这种想法。“我有过从早上八点一直工作到晚上十一点甚至凌晨一点的时候，”他说。他表示，去年有一段时间“简直是场噩梦……我每天都要工作到十一点，却不知道为什么会这样。”

Once, he tells me, at eight in the evening, he was sent a 1,000-plus-page book that had to be finished by start of business the next morning. “\[I was\] just expected to be on call,” he says.他告诉我，有一次晚上八点，他收到了一本一千多页的书，要求第二天一早开工前完成。“（当时）就指望我随叫随到，”他说。

Clarke waves his hands to dispel the memory: all of that is in the past. Mostly. Episodes like that are less indicative of the industry as a whole than the famously abrasive type it has long yielded and just as long lampooned: the showbiz kingmaker. “He was the only person who was like that.” After ten years of work for Producer X, Clarke’s reliability found him one of that person’s longest-tenured staffers. It is not a big leap to suspect that, alongside dedication to his work and acumen, Clarke was able to absorb that kind of unreasonable demand for so long because of who he is, the way he moves through the world. It’s that unassuming quality that makes him so easy to talk to. A teddy bear can be pummeled without losing its shape.克拉克挥了挥手，驱散那段回忆：一切都已成过去。至少大多如此。这样的经历，与其说是整个行业的写照，不如说是该行业长期以来盛产、也一直被嘲讽的那种出了名的强硬派——娱乐圈定局者。“他是唯一一个那样的人。”为X制片人工作十年后，克拉克的可靠让他成了对方任职时间最长的员工之一。不难推测，除了对工作的投入和敏锐的洞察力，克拉克之所以能长期承受那种无理的要求，源于他的本性、他为人处世的方式。正是这种谦逊的特质，让他与人相处格外融洽。就像一只泰迪熊，即便被揉捏也不会变形。

Producer X is quietly making moves to re-enter the industry. He calls Clarke, but Clarke doesn’t answer. His corner of the industry, Clarke says, is “a lot better now. Most people are concerned for your time… I usually have two days to read a book. Sometimes more. Most people I work for now are relaxed.” That is, most of the time. “Unless it’s a major book or there’s a bidding war or something.” 制片人X正悄悄采取行动，试图重返行业。他给克拉克打了电话，但克拉克没有接。克拉克说，他在行业里的那片天地“现在好多了。大多数人都在为你的时间操心……我通常有两天时间看书，有时候还不止两天。我现在合作的大多数客户都很放松。”大多数时候确实如此。“除非是重要的书籍，或者出现了竞标大战之类的情况。”

The 1000-page night sits on the outer limit of Clarke’s professional life, but identifying that margin is illuminating. The work is freelance and deadline-driven, with no salary, benefits, or job security. Once a book is sighted for adaptation, it has to be field-dressed. Some of the visceral mechanisms that gave it life have to be discarded. “The fear I have is that some author will read this and be horrified that something is being read in three hours,” Clarke says. “I do understand that. But I do still have to read it thoroughly and give real consideration.” 这本长达一千页的书稿处于克拉克职业生涯的边缘地带，而厘清这一边界颇具启发意义。这份工作属于自由职业，以截止日期为导向，没有薪水、福利和工作保障。一旦一本书确定要改编，就必须对其进行“实地改编”。一些赋予作品生命力的核心内核必须被舍弃。“我担心的是，有些作者看到改编本后，会因为有人只用三小时就读懂他们的作品而感到震惊，”克拉克说，“我完全理解这种感受。但我仍然必须通读全书，并给出切实的考量。”

In fact, he tells me that he tends to read for work with more intensity than he would for pleasure. Then again, the industry’s machinery demands that books are “being sent to a freelancer. You have to do it as quickly as you can. You have to do as much as you can.” Fortunately for the producers who hire him and the authors whose work he covers, Clarke brings enough sensitivity to bear that writing itself remains part of the equation. That can’t be taken for granted. Anyone who’s seen enough adaptations knows the difference between one that strives to apprehend the source material and one that feels derived from a Wikipedia summary.事实上，他告诉我，他为工作读书时的专注程度往往比为消遣读书时更高。不过，这个行业的运作机制要求书籍“必须交给自由撰稿人处理”。“你必须尽快完成，必须尽最大努力去做。”对雇佣他的制片人和他所评论的作者们来说，幸运的是，克拉克投入了足够的敏锐度，使得撰写评论本身也成为了工作的一部分。这一点绝非理所当然。任何看过足够多改编作品的人都清楚，那些力求领会原著精髓的改编，与那些看似只是照搬维基百科简介的改编，有着天壤之别。

As long as humanity is still valuable to the process of adaptation, Clarke will have work. That doesn’t mean he’s whipping around the Hollywood Hills in a convertible or power-lunching. Clarke lives in Gowanus, Brooklyn’s mostly industrial neighborhood. His home office is a narrow two-bedroom near an asphalt processing plant and a superfund site, both of which can be smelled with the windows open on a warm day. “Because of what I do and because I tip well, I think people have an inflated idea of how much money I make,” he says. “It’s volume.” I’ve seen him when things turned dire. When the notoriously fragile film industry’s gears stopped turning for one reason or another, he genuinely lost sleep, confidence, some sense of purpose. How would he pay his rent? Would he have to pack up more than two decades of work and life and move back to Delaware? All I could do at times like that was listen and discount his bar tab. But for right now, at least, the lights are on and the mill is humming.只要人类在适应进程中仍有价值，克拉克就有活可干。但这并不意味着他开着敞篷车在好莱坞山兜风，或是忙着参加高能商务午餐。克拉克住在布鲁克林的高瓦纳斯区，这是一个以工业为主的地方。他的家办公室是一间狭窄的两居室，挨着一家沥青加工厂和一个超级基金污染场地，天气暖和的时候，开着窗就能闻到两地的气味。“因为我的工作性质，再加上我小费给得大方，我觉得大家都高估了我的收入，”他说，“全靠走量。”我见过他陷入困境的样子。当以脆弱著称的电影行业因为各种原因停摆时，他真的彻夜难眠，失去了自信，也失去了部分人生目标。他该怎么付房租？是不是得收拾二十多年的工作与生活，搬回特拉华州？那些时候，我能做的只有倾听，还帮他减免酒钱。但至少眼下，灯是亮着的，工厂也在运转。

It’s little wonder producers and executives expect to get what they want when they want it. Their profession alchemizes money and labor into art, then back to money. Failing to find the requisite components in one place, they’ll look another. But they won’t stop anywhere they don’t need to, and they won’t waste time waiting for anyone. That leaves Clarke little latitude to pace his workload. “For ten years I only dated for a year during that whole period of time,” he says. “The person I dated, it was very frustrating for them. You’re never available because you’re always working.” 难怪制片人和高管们希望想要什么就能得到什么，想要什么时候得到就能什么时候得到。他们的职业将金钱和劳动力炼化为艺术，再变回金钱。如果在一个地方找不到所需的要素，他们就会换个地方找。但他们不会在不必要的地方停留，也不会浪费时间等待任何人。这让克拉克几乎没有余地来规划自己的工作量。“十年来，我在那段时间里只谈过一年恋爱，”他说，“和我交往的人都觉得特别沮丧。你永远都没空，因为你总是在工作。”

And this is fundamentally solitary work. Clarke spent much of his 30s exhausted, either from chopping away or dreading that he would never get that chance again. I saw it from behind the bar: Clarke was lonely, frustrated, and disconnected but would nonetheless listen with real generosity to whoever occupied the barstool beside him. At least for a while. He is, after all, a New Yorker.而这本质上是一项孤独的工作。克拉克三十多岁的大部分时间里都身心俱疲，要么是因为不停地砍削，要么是因为担心自己再也不会有这样的机会了。我从吧台后面看到：克拉克孤独、沮丧，与外界格格不入，但无论如何，他都会真诚而慷慨地倾听坐在旁边吧台凳上的每一个人。至少有那么一段时间是这样的。毕竟，他是个纽约人。

So when Clarke asked what I thought of *Train Dreams*, what I didn’t say was that the book had left me moved and heartbroken in a way that resisted explanation, that the feeling stayed with me more than any fully formed thought. The adaptation, which I liked, simply didn’t leave that impression. I hadn’t expected it to. Still, I felt foolish for being unable to articulate exactly what had been lost in the gulf between the two realizations of Robert Granier’s lonely life. And it occurred to me: Clarke rarely has the luxury of uncertainty.所以当克拉克问我对《火车梦》的看法时，我没有说的是，这本书以一种难以言喻的方式让我既感动又心碎，那种感觉比任何清晰的想法都更长久地留在我心中。我喜欢的改编版并没有留下这样的印象。我本就没指望它会。尽管如此，我还是为无法准确说出在两种对罗伯特·格拉尼孤独人生的演绎之间所缺失的东西而感到懊恼。我突然意识到：克拉克很少有机会沉浸在这种不确定之中。

He is now happier than I have ever known him—he’s in a healthy relationship. He tells me that his partner goes out of their way to demonstrate how much they appreciate him. He practically swoons when he tells me that they bought tickets to the 70mm IMAX screening of *One Battle After Another*. The other day, he posted a photo on social-media showing the two of them playing hooky from work, smiling in the sun-splashed theater lobby. The nature of his work and keeping himself valuable in his industry—that still requires that he pick up the axe and keep swinging. But it seems like now, if only briefly, he can stop and take a breath and just admire the forest around him.他现在比我以往任何时候都要开心——他拥有一段健康的感情。他告诉我，他的伴侣总是不遗余力地表达对他的珍视。当他说起对方为他买了《一场接一场》70毫米IMAX放映场的票时，整个人都陶醉不已。前几天，他在社交媒体上发了一张照片，照片里他和伴侣双双翘班，在洒满阳光的影院大堂里笑容满面。他的工作性质以及在行业中保持自身价值的需求——仍需要他时刻保持干劲、持续拼搏。但如今，即便只是短暂的片刻，他似乎也能停下脚步喘口气，好好欣赏身边的美好风景。

I asked Clarke if he liked *Train Dreams* and he didn’t hesitate: “I liked it a lot. I love that it was about loneliness. That everybody is alone but there can be a brief period of time when you might love somebody, and that is what makes life worthwhile.” 我问克拉克是否喜欢《火车梦》，他毫不犹豫地回答：“我非常喜欢。我喜欢这本书讲述的是孤独。每个人都是孤独的，但或许会有一段短暂的时光，你会深爱着某个人，而正是这份爱，让生命变得有意义。”

I paid the tab and walked home, thinking about what he’d said. I watched the movie again. This time, by the end, I was tearing up.我付了账单走回家，心里想着他说的话。我又看了一遍那部电影。这一次，看到最后，我热泪盈眶。
