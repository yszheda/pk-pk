## The worthlessness of vitamin D is mildly exaggerated维生素D并非一无是处，这一说法略有夸大

[dynomight](https://dynomight.net/) · · [science](https://dynomight.net/#science) [health](https://dynomight.net/#health) [effort](https://dynomight.net/#effort) [dynomight](https://dynomight.net/) ·· [科学](https://dynomight.net/#science) [健康](https://dynomight.net/#health) [研究](https://dynomight.net/#effort)

For a while there, many people thought vitamin D was magical—that it could improve bones, the heart, infections, cancer, heart disease, longevity, even mental health. But among people I respect, opinion is now overwhelmingly that taking vitamin D does nothing unless you’re severely deficient. The central argument is that while vitamin D levels are correlated with ~all positive health outcomes, when you actually test vitamin D supplements against placebo in randomized trials, nothing ever happens.曾有一段时间，很多人认为维生素D具有神奇功效——它能改善骨骼、心脏功能，抵御感染，对抗癌症、心脏病，延长寿命，甚至能改善心理健康。但在我敬重的人当中，如今绝大多数人都认为，除非你严重缺乏维生素D，否则补充它毫无作用。核心观点是，尽管维生素D水平与几乎所有积极的健康结果都存在关联，但在随机对照试验中，当你实际将维生素D补充剂与安慰剂进行对比测试时，却发现它没有任何效果。

That’s what I used to think, too. But I’ve come to think the skeptics have over-corrected. Yes, randomized trials have shown the magical correlations are not causal. But if you start with non-insane expectations, the trials look like weak but positive evidence. And if you consider what we know about biology and evolution, I think the balance of evidence tips pretty clearly in the direction that people with low-ish levels would be wise to supplement.我过去也这么认为。但我现在觉得怀疑者们矫枉过正了。没错，随机试验已经表明那些神奇的相关性并非因果关系。但如果抱着合理的预期来看，这些试验提供的证据虽不充分却仍有积极意义。而且结合我们对生物学和进化论的了解，我认为证据的倾向非常明显：体内相关营养素水平偏低的人群补充营养素是明智之举。

Am I certain that vitamin D is beneficial for people with low-ish levels? Absolutely not! But I claim that’s the best bet given the limits of our knowledge.我能确定维生素D对维生素D水平偏低的人有益吗？绝对不能！但我认为，鉴于我们现有认知的局限性，这是最稳妥的选择。

## The classical view: Boring bone vitamin经典观点：普通的骨骼维生素

Most vitamins are “ingredients” that the body uses to do stuff. Vitamin D is more like a “signal” that the body uses to communicate with itself about *what* to do.大多数维生素都是身体用来完成各项生理功能的“原料”。而维生素D更像是一种“信号”，身体用它来向自身传递指令。 [^1] The classical “endocrine” story of vitamin D is that your body uses it to tell your guts to take in more calcium from food. If you don’t get enough vitamin D, then you have calcium problems.维生素D经典的“内分泌”机制是，身体会利用它来指示肠道从食物中吸收更多钙。如果体内维生素D不足，就会出现钙相关的问题。

That’s all you really need to know about the classical view. But if you enjoy gawking at biology’s complexity, I recommend this diagram and the following three paragraphs:关于经典观点，你真正需要了解的就这些。但如果你喜欢惊叹于生物学的复杂性，我推荐这张图表以及接下来的三个段落：

[![](https://dynomight.net/img/vitamin-d/pathways.svg)](https://dynomight.net/img/vitamin-d/pathways.pdf)

Ready for science? OK: Almost all the cells in your body make **provitamin D**.准备好了解科学知识了吗？好的：你体内几乎所有的细胞都会生成 **维生素D原** 。 [^2] Usually, this is all converted to cholesterol, but your skin cells leave some sitting around. When UVB light hits those skin cells, provitamin D is transformed (physically by the light itself) into **previtamin D** and then (by heat) into **vitamin D**. This diffuses from the skin cells into blood vessels. There it binds to a protein 通常情况下，这些物质都会转化为胆固醇，但你的皮肤细胞会残留一部分。当中波紫外线照射到这些皮肤细胞时，维生素原D会被（直接由光线物理作用）转化为 **前维生素D** ，随后（通过热量作用）转化为 **维生素D** 。它从皮肤细胞扩散到血管中，并在那里与一种蛋白质结合。 [^3] and starts circulating in the blood, where it is joined by vitamin D from food.并开始在血液中循环，与此同时来自食物的维生素D也会加入其中。 [^4] Eventually, the liver converts it into more-stable **storage vitamin D**. It also soaks in and out of fat and muscle tissue, which acts as a slow-release reservoir.最终，肝脏会将其转化为更稳定的 **储存型维生素D** 。它还会在脂肪和肌肉组织中进出，充当缓释储备库。

Now, a fun fact: If calcium levels in your blood get too low, then your heart will stop working and you will die. To avoid this, you have **parathyroid glands** in your neck that sense when calcium is getting low, and release **parathyroid hormone** into the blood. This tells your bones to release some of their stored calcium. It also tells your **kidneys** to convert some of the storage vitamin D from your blood into **active vitamin D**. And when *that* gets to your guts, they try to absorb more calcium from food.现在来分享一个有趣的事实：如果血液中的钙含量过低，你的心脏就会停止跳动，你也会因此死亡。为了避免这种情况，你的颈部有 **甲状旁腺** ，它能感知钙含量何时降低，并向血液中释放 **甲状旁腺激素** 。这会指示骨骼释放部分储存的钙，同时也会让你的 **肾脏** 将血液中储存的部分维生素D转化为 **活性维生素D** 。当 *这种活性维生素D* 到达肠道时，肠道会努力从食物中吸收更多的钙。

So what happens if you don’t get enough vitamin D? Well, your body is not going to let calcium levels drop too low, because your body is designed to avoid death. Parathyroid hormone will still get secreted, and it will still tell your bones to scavenge calcium. But without vitamin D, your guts never get the signal to gather extra calcium from food. So the body scavenges a *lot* of calcium from your bones, and you end up with weak bones, which is bad.那如果你没有摄入足够的维生素D会发生什么呢？好吧，你的身体不会让钙水平降得太低，因为身体的本能就是避免死亡。甲状旁腺激素仍会分泌，它也会依旧向你的骨骼发出信号，让骨骼去摄取钙。但如果没有维生素D，肠道就永远收不到从食物中摄取额外钙的信号。于是身体会从你的骨骼中摄取 *大量* 的钙，最终导致骨骼变脆弱，这可不是好事。

Now here’s the thing: In this story, only active vitamin D actually does anything. The kidneys make this on demand in response to calcium levels, *not* in response to storage vitamin D levels. [General opinion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5393439) is that as long as the blood has above ~25 nmol/L of storage vitamin D, then the kidneys have no trouble making active vitamin D.事情是这样的：在这个说法里，只有活性维生素D真正发挥作用。肾脏会根据钙水平按需生成这种活性维生素D， *而非* 根据储存型维生素D的水平来生成。 [普遍观点](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5393439) 是，只要血液中储存型维生素D的含量高于约25纳摩尔/升，肾脏就能顺利生成活性维生素D。 [^5] Furthermore, [survey data](https://doi.org/10.1016/s8756-3282\(02\)00692-0) suggests that only ~2% of the population has levels below that threshold. This suggests that for ~98% of people, supplementing vitamin D should do approximately nothing.此外， [调查数据](https://doi.org/10.1016/s8756-3282\(02\)00692-0) 显示，只有约2%的人群维生素D水平低于该阈值。这意味着对约98%的人来说，补充维生素D几乎不会有任何效果。

## The correlation view: Magical mystery cure相关性观点：神奇的神秘疗法

Rickets is a terrible disease that involves soft bones, stunted growth, and skeletal deformities. It’s probably been with us since ancient times, but it became common in the West after the industrial revolution. In 1890, a Scottish missionary named Theobald Palm [observed](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=pst.32239000047437&seq=288) that rickets was common in smog-ridden UK cities but almost unheard of in sunny countries with poor sanitation, suggesting sunlight itself was the issue. This contributed to the discovery that rickets could be cured with UV light or cod-liver oil, and eventually the discovery of vitamin D.佝偻病是一种可怕的疾病，会导致骨骼软化、发育迟缓以及骨骼畸形。这种病或许自古就存在，但在工业革命后于西方变得普遍。1890年，一位名叫西奥博尔德·帕尔姆的苏格兰传教士\*\*发现\*\*，佝偻病在烟雾弥漫的英国城市十分常见，而在卫生条件差的阳光充足的国家却几乎闻所未闻，这表明阳光本身是关键因素。这一发现推动了佝偻病可通过紫外线或鱼肝油治愈的研究，最终促成了维生素D的发现。

In 1941, Apperly [noticed](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerres/article/1/3/191/472224/The-Relation-of-Solar-Radiation-to-Cancer) that the amount of sunlight in different US states was positively correlated with skin cancer but *inversely* correlated with overall cancer mortality.1941年，阿珀利 [注意到](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerres/article/1/3/191/472224/The-Relation-of-Solar-Radiation-to-Cancer) 美国不同州的日照时长与皮肤癌呈正相关，但与总体癌症死亡率呈 *负* 相关。 [^6] He gave this charming graph: 他给出了这幅有趣的图表：

![](https://dynomight.net/img/vitamin-d/apperly-rot.png)

Apperly never mentions vitamin D, presumably because he thought it was a boring bone vitamin.阿珀利从未提及维生素D，想必是因为他觉得这是一种不起眼的补骨维生素。

Things took off in 1980, when Cedric and Frank Garland published, [“Do Sunlight and Vitamin D Reduce the Likelihood of Colon Cancer?”](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/9.3.227) Seemingly unaware of Apperly, they gave a similar, but uglier, graph:1980年情况迎来转机，塞德里克·加兰与弗兰克·加兰二人发表了《阳光与维生素D是否会降低结直肠癌的患病风险？》一文。他们似乎并不知晓阿珀利的研究，却给出了一张相似但形式更粗糙的图表：

![](https://dynomight.net/img/vitamin-d/garland.png)

They point out that regional diets (like meat and fiber) didn’t seem to explain this pattern. Instead, they propose a mechanistic story:他们指出，地区性饮食（如肉类和纤维）似乎无法解释这一模式。相反，他们提出了一个机制性解释：

Sunlight 阳光  
↓  
Vitamin D 维生素D  
↓  
Adequate calcium in blood 血液中充足的钙  
↓  
Reduced inflammation of epithelial cells in the colon 减少结肠上皮细胞的炎症  
↓  
Less colon cancer 患结肠癌的风险更低

(It’s always inflammation.) This paper was rejected many times before finally being published. I wish I could find an un-gated copy to link to, because it would have made a magnificent blog post.（问题总是出在炎症上。）这篇论文在最终发表前被拒稿了很多次。真希望我能找到一份开放获取的版本来附上链接，因为它本可以写成一篇精彩的博客文章。 [^7]

Following that paper, there was an explosion of work that found negative correlations between sunlight (or latitude) and other types of [cancers](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2012.11.001) as well as [blood pressure](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.HYP.30.2.150), [diabetes](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-008-1061-5), and [multiple sclerosis](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2011.240432).在那篇论文之后，大量研究涌现，发现阳光（或纬度）与其他类型的 [癌症](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2012.11.001) 以及 [血压](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.HYP.30.2.150) 、 [糖尿病](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-008-1061-5) 和 [多发性硬化症](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2011.240432) 之间存在负相关关系。

Then people started measuring vitamin D in blood. In 1989, the Garlands and collaborators [found](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736\(89\)91789-3) blood samples takin in 1974 from 25,000 people. They found that 34 of those people had since gotten colon cancer. They matched these with 67 demographically similar people and measured vitamin D levels in the stored blood samples for all 101 people. Among that group, people with vitamin D levels below 50 nmol/L got colon cancer more than three times as often as people with higher levels.后来人们开始检测血液中的维生素D。1989年，加兰夫妇及其合作者 [发现](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736\(89\)91789-3) 了1974年采集的2.5万人的血液样本。他们发现其中34人后来患上了结肠癌。研究人员将这些人与67名人口统计学特征相似的人进行匹配，并对这101人的储存血液样本中的维生素D水平进行了检测。在该群体中，维生素D水平低于50纳摩尔/升的人患结肠癌的几率是水平较高者的三倍多。

Again, many similar studies followed. These linked higher vitamin D levels to better outcomes in cardiovascular disease, diabetes, obesity, infectious disease, Parkinson’s, and mood disorders. While results were mixed for non-colorectal cancer incidence, higher vitamin D levels predicted better survival of many cancers. Amazingly, *all-cause mortality* was roughly 30% lower for those at the 75th percentile of vitamin D levels compared to the 25th.此后又有许多类似研究相继开展。这些研究将更高的维生素D水平与心血管疾病、糖尿病、肥胖症、传染病、帕金森病以及情绪障碍的更好预后关联起来。尽管在非结直肠癌的发病率方面研究结果不一，但更高的维生素D水平预示着多种癌症患者的生存率更高。令人惊讶的是，维生素D水平处于75百分位的人群，其全因死亡率比处于25百分位的人群低约30%。

Vitamin D was looking like a miracle. But how could it do all that stuff if it was just a boring bone vitamin?维生素D曾被视为一种奇迹物质。但如果它只是一种不起眼的骨骼维生素，又怎么能发挥如此多的功效呢？

## Meanwhile in biology 与此同时，在生物学领域

While all these correlations were being discovered, we learned that the body doesn’t just use vitamin D for bone stuff.在发现所有这些相关性的同时，我们了解到人体并不仅仅是为了骨骼相关的生理功能而利用维生素D。

In 1969, we discovered the [vitamin D receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D_receptor) that active vitamin D binds to in the gut and bones. And in the 1980s came a shock: Almost all cells in the body have vitamin D receptors. These seem to do different things in different tissues. In the pancreas, they support insulin secretion. In immune cells, they boost antimicrobial peptides and reduce inflammation. In neurons, they influence proliferation and differentiation.1969年，我们发现了活性维生素D在肠道和骨骼中结合的 [维生素D受体](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D_receptor) 。到了20世纪80年代，一个令人震惊的发现出现了：人体几乎所有细胞都拥有维生素D受体。这些受体在不同组织中似乎发挥着不同的作用。在胰腺中，它们支持胰岛素分泌；在免疫细胞中，它们能增强抗菌肽的产生并减轻炎症；在神经元中，它们会影响细胞的增殖与分化。

So… What? When calcium drops and the kidneys put out active vitamin D, does every part of the body start doing different unrelated stuff?那么……怎么回事？当血钙下降，肾脏分泌活性维生素D时，身体的各个部位是不是就开始做各种毫无关联的事情了？

In the late 1990s, we cloned the gene for the [enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25-Hydroxyvitamin_D_1-alpha-hydroxylase) that the kidneys use to convert storage vitamin D to active vitamin D. Soon came another shock: This enzyme also exists in tons of other cells, including immune cells, the heart, the skin, the prostate, the breast, and colon. (Another win for the Garlands.) 20世纪90年代末，我们克隆出了肾脏用于将储存型维生素D转化为活性维生素D的酶\</b>的基因。很快又有一个令人震惊的发现：这种酶还存在于众多其他细胞中，包括免疫细胞、心脏、皮肤、前列腺、乳腺和结肠。（这又是加兰夫妇的一大成就。）

So it’s not just the kidneys making active vitamin D to trigger the gut. Cells everywhere are making their *own* active vitamin D and using it to trigger vitamin D receptors in neighboring cells, or even inside the same cell.所以，不只是肾脏会制造活性维生素D来作用于肠道。各处的细胞都会制造自身的活性维生素D，并利用它来激活邻近细胞甚至同一细胞内的维生素D受体。 [^8] This often has little to do with calcium or bones.这通常与钙或骨骼关系不大。 [^9]

So: 那么：

1. The kidneys use vitamin D as a boring bone hormone.肾脏将维生素D当作一种普通的骨骼激素来使用。
2. As long as the blood contains at least ~25 nmol/L of storage vitamin D, the kidneys don’t care. They create the same amount of active vitamin D, in response to calcium levels.只要血液中储存的维生素D含量至少达到约25纳摩尔/升，肾脏就不会有任何区别对待。它们会根据钙的水平，生成等量的活性维生素D。
3. But now cells everywhere are using storage vitamin D.但如今，身体各处的细胞都在利用储备型维生素D。
4. To do god-knows-what. 去做些无人知晓的事。
5. With god-knows-what sensitivity to circulating vitamin D levels.对循环维生素D水平的敏感度也各不相同。

And remember how only active vitamin D does anything? That’s wrong. In the mid-1970s, we learned that *storage* vitamin D also binds to the vitamin D receptor. The affinity is 100-1000× lower, but have ~1000× more in your blood. So maybe circulating levels of storage vitamin D themselves matter, independently of how much active vitamin D gets made?还记得只有活性维生素D才起作用吗？这个说法是错的。20世纪70年代中期，我们了解到 *储存型* 维生素D也能与维生素D受体结合。其亲和力低100至1000倍，但在血液中的含量却高出约1000倍。那么，储存型维生素D的循环水平本身是否也有影响，且与活性维生素D的生成量无关呢？

If that’s not confusing enough, people also noticed that while active vitamin D levels in the blood aren’t correlated with storage vitamin D (above ~25 nmol/L), levels of *parathyroid hormone* (the thing your parathyroid glands use to tell your kidneys to make active vitamin D) seem to decline as levels of storage vitamin D rise from ~25 to 50 or 75 nmol/L. Huh?如果这还不够让人困惑，人们还发现，虽然血液中的活性维生素D水平与储备维生素D（约25纳摩尔/升以上）并无关联，但当储备维生素D水平从约25纳摩尔/升上升至50或75纳摩尔/升时， *甲状旁腺激素* （甲状旁腺用来指示肾脏生成活性维生素D的物质）的水平似乎会下降。懂了吗？ [^10]

On the one hand, all this makes the idea that vitamin D could be a miracle more plausible. On the other hand, this is getting complicated. And do we really believe that raising your vitamin D levels from the 25th to the 75th percentile could reduce your risk of death from *any cause* by *thirty percent*? Maybe we should try giving people vitamin D and see what happens.一方面，所有这些都让维生素D可能是一种奇迹成分的说法变得更有说服力。另一方面，情况也变得愈发复杂。我们真的相信将维生素D水平从第25百分位提升到第75百分位，就能将 *任何原因* 导致的死亡风险降低 *30%* 吗？或许我们应该给人们补充维生素D，看看结果如何。

## Then came the RCTs 随机对照试验接踵而至

There have been *many* randomized trials. The “right” thing to do in such cases is to look at meta analyses that carefully combine all the data. We’ll get to those. But they conceal a lot of important nuance about what actually happens on the ground during these trials. So let’s start by going over the three main “megatrials”.已经开展了 *大量* 随机对照试验。在这类情况下，正确的做法是查看仔细整合了所有数据的荟萃分析。我们后续会谈到这些分析，但它们掩盖了试验期间实际发生的诸多重要细节。因此，我们先梳理三大“大型试验”。

The Women’s Health Initiative (WHI) trial came out in 2006 and is still the largest vitamin D trial ever done. This took 36,000 postmenopausal American women and assigned half to take 400 IU daily with calcium and the other half to placebo.妇女健康倡议（WHI）试验于2006年公布，仍是迄今为止规模最大的维生素D试验。该试验招募了3.6万名美国绝经后女性，将其中一半受试者每日补充400国际单位维生素D并搭配钙，另一半则服用安慰剂。 [^11] After seven years, here’s what happened:七年后，结果如下： [^12]

| Outcome (WHI trial) 结局（WHI 试验） | Hazard ratio 风险比 |
| --- | --- |
| Fractures 骨折 | 0.97 (0.91 to 1.03) 0.97（0.91至1.03） |
| Cancer 癌症 | 0.97 (0.91 to 1.04) 0.97（0.91至1.04） |
| Cancer mortality 癌症死亡率 | 0.90 (0.77 to 1.05) 0.90（0.77至1.05） |
| CVD mortality 心血管疾病死亡率 | 0.94 (0.78 to 1.12) 0.94（0.78至1.12） |
| All-cause mortality 全因死亡率 | 0.92 (0.83 to 1.01) 0.92（0.83 至 1.01） |
| Kidney stones 肾结石 | 1.17 (1.02 to 1.34) 1.17（1.02至1.34） |

(The [hazard ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_ratio) is the ratio of the rate that something happens in the treatment vs. placebo groups. So, a number less than one suggests a benefit to taking vitamin D, while a number larger than one suggests a harm. The numbers in parentheses show a 95% confidence interval.) （ [风险比](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_ratio) 指的是某一事件在治疗组与安慰剂组中的发生概率之比。因此，数值小于1表明服用维生素D有益，而数值大于1则表明存在风险。括号中的数值表示95%置信区间。）

The only statistically significant result was a bad one: Extra kidney stones, likely from the extra calcium.唯一具有统计学意义的结果是个负面结论：额外的钙很可能导致了更多肾结石的形成。 [^13] The other outcomes look vaguely good, but none were statistically significant despite the massive sample size.其他结果看起来大致不错，但尽管样本量巨大，没有一项具有统计学显著性。

This was disappointing. However, the WHI trial had limitations: Many subjects in both the vitamin D and placebo groups were already taking vitamin D, and continued taking it through the trial. The dose of 400 IU was fairly low, many subjects stopped taking their pills, and vitamin D levels didn’t actually change that much. They also measured vitamin D levels in only 6% of subjects, meaning we can’t compare the fates of subjects who started out with low versus high levels.这一结果令人失望。然而，妇女健康倡议（WHI）试验存在局限性：维生素D组和安慰剂组的许多受试者原本就在服用维生素D，且在整个试验期间仍持续服用。400国际单位的剂量相当低，许多受试者停止服药，且维生素D水平实际上并未发生太大变化。他们还仅对6%的受试者测量了维生素D水平，这意味着我们无法比较初始维生素D水平低与高的受试者的结局。

The next big hope was VITAL, which came out in 2018. They recruited 26,000 older people across the United States, half of them men and 20% Black (and thus [far more likely](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2025.09.046) to be vitamin-D deficient). They measured vitamin D levels in most people, and they gave the treatment group 2,000 IU per day.下一个重大希望是2018年推出的VITAL研究。该研究在美国招募了26000名老年人，其中一半为男性，20%为黑人（因此 [维生素D缺乏的可能性要大得多](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2025.09.046) ）。研究人员测量了大多数受试者的维生素D水平，并为治疗组每天提供2000国际单位的维生素D。 [^14] Here were the results after 5.3 years:5.3年后的结果如下：

| --- | --- |
| Diabetes 糖尿病 | 0.91 (0.76 to 1.09) 0.91（0.76至1.09） |
| Autoimmune disease 自身免疫性疾病 | [0.78](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2021-066452) (0.61 to 0.99) [0.78](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2021-066452) (0.61 至 0.99) |
| Cancer 癌症 | 0.96 (0.88 to 1.06) 0.96（0.88至1.06） |
| Cancer mortality 癌症死亡率 | 0.83 (0.67 to 1.02) 0.83（0.67至1.02） |
| Major CVD event 主要心血管事件 | 0.97 (0.85 to 1.12) 0.97（0.85至1.12） |
| CVD mortality 心血管疾病死亡率 | 1.11 (0.88 to 1.40) 1.11（0.88至1.40） |
| All-cause mortality 全因死亡率 | 0.99 (0.87 to 1.12) 0.99（0.87至1.12） |

Some of the results look good-ish, but cardiovascular mortality was higher in the treatment group, leading to almost no effect on all-cause mortality.部分结果看起来还算不错，但治疗组的心血管死亡率更高，这使得其对全因死亡率几乎没有影响。 [^15] More disappointment. 更令人失望。

The last megatrial was D-Health, which came out in 2022 based on 21,000 older Australians. Instead of daily supplements, it used a monthly “bolus” dose of 60,000 IU or placebo. Unlike in VITAL, there was no exclusion for people with a history of cardiovascular disease or cancer, and less restriction on how much vitamin D participants could take on their own during the trial.最后一项大型临床试验是D-Health，该试验于2022年开展，研究对象为2.1万名澳大利亚老年人。该试验未采用每日补充剂的方式，而是使用每月一次60000国际单位的“大剂量”维生素D或安慰剂。与VITAL试验不同，该试验未将有心血管疾病或癌症病史的人群排除在外，且对参与者在试验期间自行服用维生素D的剂量限制也更为宽松。 [^16] Here were the results after 6 years:以下是6年后的研究结果：

| CVD mortality 心血管疾病死亡率 | 0.96 (0.72 to 1.28) 0.96（0.72至1.28） |

Now, the treatment group did better in terms of cardiovascular disease, but worse in cancer and worse in all-cause mortality. Even more disappointment.现在，治疗组在心血管疾病方面表现更好，但在癌症方面以及全因死亡率方面表现更差。结果更令人失望。

Just from these three large trials, the main lesson should already be clear: Vitamin D is not a miracle. The correlations were wrong.仅从这三项大型试验来看，核心结论应该已经很明确了：维生素D并非灵丹妙药。相关的关联是错误的。 [^17] There is essentially zero remaining hope that taking vitamin D could reduce all-cause mortality by a third.补充维生素D能将全因死亡率降低三分之一的希望，如今基本已化为泡影。

In this sense, the vitamin D skeptics are definitely right. But what about the other trials? And is there a more subtle lesson?从这个意义上说，维生素D怀疑论者无疑是正确的。但其他那些试验又该如何看待？其中是否还藏着更深层的启示？

## Squinting at the data 细看这些数据

There are various ways you could try to squint at these RCT. In almost all of them, most people already had pretty high levels before they started. So why don’t we separate out people who started low? Usually we can’t, because most trials didn’t measure baseline vitamin D.你可以从多种角度来解读这些随机对照试验（RCT）。但在几乎所有研究中，大多数人在参与试验前体内的维生素D水平就已经相当高了。那我们为什么不把初始水平较低的人群单独分出来分析呢？通常我们做不到，因为大多数试验都没有测量受试者的维生素D基线水平。 [^20] And among the trials that did, there are few people with low levels, so the results are noisy and confusing.而在确实开展了的试验中，低剂量组的参与者寥寥无几，因此试验结果杂乱且令人困惑。 [^21]

Or, you might theorize that benefits would take time to show up, meaning the first couple years just add noise. In some cases—notably VITAL—excluding the first two years seems to help, but in other cases things get worse.或者，你可以提出这样的理论：效益需要时间才能显现，也就是说最初的几年只会带来干扰。在某些情况下——尤其是VITAL项目——排除前两年的数据似乎会有所帮助，但在另一些情况下，结果反而会变得更糟。 [^22]

Finally, some people speculate that taking gigantic monthly or quarterly “bolus” doses of vitamin D might be dangerous. For example, here’s an enjoyable paragraph from Kunzia et al. in their meta-analysis of vitamin D and cancer mortality:最后，一些人推测每月或季度大剂量“单次冲击”补充维生素D可能存在风险。例如，以下是Kunzia等人在其关于维生素D与癌症死亡率的荟萃分析中一段有趣的内容：

> Our results showing efficacy of daily, but not bolus, vitamin D3 supplementation in reducing cancer mortality are consistent with previous meta-analyses on cancer mortality or all-cause mortality (Guo et al., 2022; Keum et al., 2022; Keum et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2019). However, by including more trials than these previous meta-analyses, we were able to detect statistically significant effect modification by treatment regimen for the first time with statistical significance (pinteraction=0.042). The pattern of intake could be important for a favourable steady state of the bioavailability of the active 1,25 (OH)₂D hormone. Daily administration counteracts the fast excretion of vitamin D from the circulation (Hollis and Wagner, 2013; Keum et al., 2022). Moreover, the enzymes CYP27B1 (converts 25(OH)D to 1,25 (OH)₂D) and CYP24A1 (inactivates 25(OH)D and 1,25(OH)₂D) follow first-order reaction kinetics (Vieth, 2009). This means that doubling the concentration of the precursor doubles the yield of the product, unlike other steroid hormones (e.g., cortisol, oestrogen, testosterone) that follow zero-order kinetics (Vieth, 2020). Intermittent, non-physiologically large vitamin D3 bolus doses may lead to unstable cycling of 25(OH)D and 1,25(OH)₂D levels in blood because the system needs time to adapt to the large doses (Hollis and Wagner, 2013; Keum et al., 2019; Vieth, 2020). In the long run, intermittent bolus regimens at weekly or larger intervals can lead to an up-regulation of countervailing factors (e.g., 24-hydroxylase (CYP24A1), 24,25(OH)2D and fibroblast growth factor 23), all of which ultimately leads to lower synthesis or higher degradation of 1,25(OH)₂D levels (Mazess et al., 2021). Bolus doses, unlike daily doses, failed to reduce C-reactive protein response and actually elevated anti-inflammatory cytokines and doubled the risk of hypercalcemia in previous studies (Krishnan et al., 2012; Martineau et al., 2017; Mazess et al., 2021).我们的研究结果显示，每日而非大剂量补充维生素D3可降低癌症死亡率，这与以往关于癌症死亡率或全因死亡率的荟萃分析结果一致（Guo等人，2022；Keum等人，2022；Keum等人，2019；Zhang等人，2022；Zhang等人，2019）。然而，通过纳入比以往这些荟萃分析更多的试验，我们首次在统计学上显著检测到了治疗方案对效应的显著修饰作用（交互作用p值=0.042）。摄入模式对于活性1,25(OH)₂D激素生物利用度的稳定稳态可能至关重要。每日给药可抵消维生素D从血液循环中的快速排泄（Hollis & Wagner，2013；Keum等人，2022）。此外，CYP27B1酶（将25(OH)D转化为1,25(OH)₂D）和CYP24A1酶（使25(OH)D和1,25(OH)₂D失活）遵循一级反应动力学（Vieth，2009）。这意味着前体浓度加倍会使产物产量加倍，而其他类固醇激素（如皮质醇、雌激素、睾酮）则遵循零级反应动力学（Vieth，2020）。间歇性、非生理剂量的大剂量维生素D3冲击给药可能导致血液中25(OH)D和1,25(OH)₂D水平的不稳定波动，因为机体需要时间来适应大剂量给药（Hollis & Wagner，2013；Keum等人，2019；Vieth，2020）。从长期来看，每周或更长间隔的间歇性冲击给药方案可能会导致代偿性因子（如24-羟化酶（CYP24A1）、24,25(OH)₂D和成纤维细胞生长因子23）上调，所有这些因素最终都会导致1,25(OH)₂D水平的合成降低或降解加快（Mazess等人，2021）。在以往的研究中，与每日剂量不同，冲击给药未能降低C反应蛋白反应，反而升高了抗炎细胞因子，并使高钙血症的风险增加一倍（Krishnan等人，2012；Martineau等人，2017；Mazess等人，2021）。

Oh no, up-regulation of fibroblast growth factor 23!糟糕，成纤维细胞生长因子23的上调！ [^23]

I don’t feel like I understand this deeply enough to have any opinion beyond the surface level that the body seems to adapt to large doses of vitamin D in ways that could possibly be bad.我觉得自己对此的理解还不够深入，除了表面上知道身体可能会以潜在有害的方式适应大剂量维生素D之外，无法形成任何观点。 [^24] It seems intuitive that small daily doses would be safer than gigantic monthly doses, but I’m always suspicious of post-hoc mechanistic speculation. Also, if people get enough sun, they can apparently synthesize 10,000-25,000 IU per day, which isn’t that far from the 60,000 IU they got in the D-Health trial. But then again, I think Kunzia et al. are suggesting that the body is designed to adapt to *regular* exposure to large doses but not *intermittent* exposure?从直觉上看，每天小剂量服用似乎比每月大剂量服用更安全，但我始终对事后的机制推测持怀疑态度。此外，如果人们接受足够的日晒，每天显然能合成 10000 至 25000 国际单位，这与 D-Health 试验中 60000 国际单位的剂量相差并不远。不过话又说回来，我认为昆齐亚等人的研究是在表明，人体的生理机制是为适应 *规律* 大剂量暴露而设计的，而非适应 *间歇性* 暴露？

## Where are we? 我们现在到哪一步了？

## The case for supplementing anyway 无论如何都要补充维生素D的理由

### It’s biologically plausible that vitamin D is good从生物学角度来看，维生素D有益是合理的推测

Everyone agrees that severe vitamin D deficiency (below ~25 nmol/L) is bad. It leads to [rickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickets), [adult rickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteomalacia), [osteoporosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoporosis), muscle weakness or even—with profound deficiency—to seizures or cardiac arrhythmia. This makes sense, because below ~25 nmol/L, the kidneys have trouble converting storage vitamin D into active vitamin D, meaning you don’t absorb enough calcium from food.所有人都一致认为，严重的维生素D缺乏症（低于约25纳摩尔/升）是有害的。它会导致 [佝偻病](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickets) 、 [成人骨软化症](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteomalacia) 、 [骨质疏松症](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoporosis) 、肌肉无力，甚至在严重缺乏的情况下引发癫痫或心律失常。这是有道理的，因为当维生素D水平低于约25纳摩尔/升时，肾脏难以将储存的维生素D转化为活性维生素D，这意味着你无法从食物中吸收足够的钙。

The question is if taking supplement to *further* raise your levels (say to 50 or 90 nmol/L) is important. We have no mechanistic proof, but it might be true, because many parts of the body use vitamin D as a local signal and because cells are at least somewhat sensitive to circulating storage levels. There’s also this weird thing where parathyroid hormone continues to decline as vitamin D levels rise above ~25 nmol/L even while this seems to make little difference to how much active vitamin D the kidneys make.问题在于，服用补充剂来 *进一步* 提高你的维生素D水平（比如达到50或90纳摩尔/升）是否重要。目前我们没有相关的机制证据，但这一说法可能成立，原因在于人体多个部位会将维生素D作为局部信号使用，且细胞对循环储存水平至少存在一定程度的敏感性。还有一个反常现象：当维生素D水平超过约25纳摩尔/升时，甲状旁腺激素会持续下降，尽管这似乎对肾脏生成的活性维生素D量几乎没有影响。

Nothing in this world comes without trade-offs. Surely, supplementing vitamin D comes with *some* downsides. But it seems very unlikely that raising vitamin D levels to a “normal” level would cause more harm than benefit. Especially because… 这个世界上没有任何事情是没有权衡取舍的。当然，补充维生素D确实存在 *一些* 弊端。但将维生素D水平提升至“正常”水平造成的危害大于益处的可能性似乎非常小。尤其是因为……

### Humans evolved to have a lot of vitamin D人类进化过程中需要大量的维生素D

According to Luxwolda et al.’s 2012 paper, [“Traditionally living populations in East Africa have a mean serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration of 115 nmol/L”](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114511007161), traditionally living populations in East Africa have a mean serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration of 115 nmol/L.根据勒克斯沃尔达等人2012年的论文， [“东非传统生活人群的血清25-羟维生素D平均浓度为115纳摩尔/升”](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114511007161) ，东非传统生活人群的血清25-羟维生素D平均浓度为115纳摩尔/升。

Meanwhile, [Wahl et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11657-012-0093-0) try to estimate mean levels around the world today:与此同时， [沃尔等人（2012年）](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11657-012-0093-0) 试图估算如今全球的平均维生素D水平：

This map looks weird because of varying lifestyle, diet, supplementation, and needing to combine fragmented studies. But you get the idea. And remember, those are just averages. So there are *lots* of people with levels *far* lower than that in our evolutionary history.这张地图看起来有些奇怪，原因是生活方式、饮食、补充剂各不相同，而且需要结合零散的研究。但大致意思就是这样。记住，这些只是平均值。所以在我们的进化史上，有 *很多* 人的相关指标水平比这个数值 *低* 得多。

Of course, just the fact that vitamin D levels have dropped doesn’t mean it’s important. Parasitic worm load, wood smoke inhalation, and cousin marriage have also dropped, but we aren’t rushing to restore those to ancestral levels.当然，仅仅是维生素D水平下降这一点并不意味着它很重要。寄生虫感染数量、吸入木烟的情况以及堂表亲通婚现象也都减少了，但我们并不会急着把这些恢复到祖先时代的水平。

But there’s another piece of evidence: After humans migrated out of East Africa, some of them evolved pale skin. Pale skin is bad, because it allows light to destroy folate, which is crucial for pregnancy.但还有另一项证据：人类迁出东非之后，部分人群进化出了浅色皮肤。浅色皮肤是不利的，因为它会让光线破坏叶酸，而叶酸对怀孕至关重要。 [^26] Evolution doesn’t typically do things that harm fertility, because evolution wants to increase reproductive fitness. The [most common explanation](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpregu.00136.2019) is that pale skin allows more UV light to penetrate, and thus allows people to synthesize more vitamin D. If evolution was willing to pay the high “price” of folate destruction for more vitamin D, that seems like good evidence that vitamin D is important.进化通常不会做出损害生育能力的行为，因为进化的目的是提高繁殖适应性。 [最常见的解释](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpregu.00136.2019) 是，白皙的皮肤能让更多紫外线穿透，从而让人体合成更多维生素D。如果进化愿意为了获取更多维生素D而付出叶酸被破坏这一高昂“代价”，这似乎就是维生素D至关重要的有力证据。

Some even see contrasts like the Inuits versus Scandinavians as a kind of natural experiment: They lived at similar latitudes, but Inuits ate a diet with vitamin D (fatty fish and whale blubber) and Scandinavians didn’t. The result is that Inuits have darker skin than Scandinavians.一些人甚至将因纽特人与斯堪的纳维亚人之间的差异视为一种自然实验：他们生活在相似的纬度，但因纽特人以富含维生素D的食物（肥鱼和鲸脂）为食，而斯堪的纳维亚人则不是。结果就是因纽特人的肤色比斯堪的纳维亚人更深。 [^27]

This is all speculative, and even if true, might be driven by severe deficiency and rickets. Or perhaps prehistoric benefits don’t translate to your lifestyle. But *all* the people in Luxwolda’s sample in East Africa had levels above ~60 nmol/L. I just don’t see how you can look at this and not see it as providing *some* suggestive evidence in favor of the idea that raising levels above severe deficiency is unlikely to be harmful, and could be important. So I think the prior is favorable.这一切都只是推测，而且即便情况属实，也可能是由严重缺乏症和佝偻病导致的。又或许史前时期的益处并不适用于你的生活方式。但卢克斯沃尔达在东非样本中的 *所有* 研究对象，其维生素D水平都高于约60纳摩尔/升。我实在不明白，看到这样的结果，怎么能不将其视为提供了 *一定* 的佐证，支持“将维生素D水平提升至严重缺乏症阈值以上不太可能造成危害，反而可能至关重要”这一观点。因此，我认为这一先验假设是成立的。

### What do you expect from vitamin D?你对维生素D有何期待？

A hazard ratio like HR = 0.96 doesn’t look very impressive. But hold on. Suppose that life expectancy is 80 years and that taking vitamin D every day reduces your risk of all-cause mortality by a factor of *HR*. A reasonable approximation in rich countries is that this would increase your life expectancy by 风险比（HR）= 0.96 这样的数值看起来并不起眼。但先别急。假设预期寿命为 80 岁，每天服用维生素 D 会使全因死亡率风险降低 *HR* 倍。在富裕国家，一个合理的近似值是，这会使你的预期寿命增加

*80 × 0.15 × (1-HR)* years *\= 12 × (1-HR)* years, *80 × 0.15 × (1-HR)* 年 *\= 12 × (1-HR)* 年

where 0.15 is derived from the entropy of lifespan in rich countries.其中0.15源自富裕国家人群寿命的熵值。 [^28] For example, if all-cause mortality had a true hazard ratio of HR = 0.96, then taking vitamin D every day of your life would increase life expectancy by around 例如，若全因死亡率的真实风险比为 HR = 0.96，那么终身每天服用维生素 D 可将预期寿命延长约

*0.48* years. *0.48* 年。

I claim that this would be a lot. Certainly, if I were about to face my destiny, I would pay a lot of money for an extra 0.48 years. Or, you can calculate that this corresponds to an increase of life expectancy *per-vitamin-D-pill* of 8.6 minutes.我认为这会是一笔不小的数目。当然，如果我即将面对自己的命运，我会花一大笔钱来换取额外的0.48年寿命。或者，你可以计算出，这相当于每服用一粒维生素D补充剂，预期寿命就能增加8.6分钟。 [^29] A common rule-of-thumb is that smoking a cigarette costs around 11 minutes of life in expectation. If you think HR = 0.96 is trivial, do you also think that smoking one cigarette each day is fine?一个常见的经验法则是，抽一支烟预计会缩短约11分钟的寿命。如果你认为风险比（HR）0.96微不足道，那你是否也觉得每天抽一支烟是没问题的？ [^30]

The correlational studies suggested that vitamin D might drop your risk of all-cause mortality by a third. It’s disappointing that the RCTs refuted this. But those correlational studies were crazy. They imply 相关研究表明，维生素D可能会将全因死亡风险降低三分之一。随机对照试验反驳了这一结论，这令人失望。但那些相关研究的结论并不严谨，它们暗示 [^31] an increase of life expectancy of around 4 years or around 6.5 cigarettes per day. Could we really believe that you could smoke 6.5 cigarettes, then take a vitamin D pill, and you’re even?预期寿命增加约4年，相当于每天多抽约6.5支香烟。我们真的能相信，每天抽6.5支烟后再吃一粒维生素D补充剂，就能抵消影响、安然无恙吗？

Personally, I think hazard ratios just slightly less than one are the best we can reasonably hope for. But I also think that they would be an *excellent* return on investment. Arguably, modern human life expectancy comes from stacking lots of modest hazard ratios on top of each other.就我个人而言，我认为风险比略小于1是我们合理范围内能期望的最佳结果。但我也认为这会是 *极佳* 的投资回报。可以说，现代人的预期寿命正是源于将众多适度的风险比层层叠加而来。

### The trials do find slightly helpful numbers这些试验确实显示出略微有利的数据

Recent meta-analyses have not consistently found a statistically significant benefit to vitamin D supplementation. That’s *something*.近期的荟萃分析并未一致发现补充维生素D具有统计学上显著的益处。但这些研究确实 *表明* 其对癌症死亡率和全因死亡率存在小幅益处，且该结果 *接近* 达到统计学显著性。这也算 *一点收获* 。

And if you buy the argument that bolus dosing is bad, the results get even better. Kunzia et al. did a meta-analysis of cancer mortality using only trials with daily dosing, and found a hazard ratio of 0.88 (confidence interval 0.78 to 0.98). I’d keep this at arm’s length. However, it’s rare in the rest of Europe (exceptions: Belgium, Poland) and even-more rare in the rest of the world (exceptions: Chile, Ethiopia, Pakistan).强化食品在英语圈和斯堪的纳维亚半岛十分常见。不过，在欧洲其他地区（比利时、波兰除外）这种食品很少见，而在世界其他地区（智利、埃塞俄比亚、巴基斯坦除外）则更为罕见。

I think this is important for two reasons. First, vitamin D is oddly self-defeating. There are some places in the world where people care about vitamin D. These are the places that run large trials. But these places also fortify their food and tend to be full of people that already supplement vitamin D. These places also tend to believe it’s unethical to tell the control group not to take vitamin D.我认为这一点很重要，原因有二。首先，维生素D的情况有些反常，反而会适得其反。世界上有一些地方的人们关注维生素D，这些地方会开展大型临床试验。但这些地方同时也会对食品进行维生素D强化，而且当地居民往往已经在补充维生素D。此外，这些地方通常还认为，告知对照组不要服用维生素D是不道德的。

And here’s another question: If you think vitamin D is worthless, are you comfortable recommending *removing* vitamin D from food? If not, then why is the particular amount of fortification in food now the right one?还有一个问题：如果你认为维生素D毫无价值，那你是否愿意建议从食物中 *去除* 维生素D？如果不愿意，那为何目前食物中特定的强化添加量就是合理的呢？

Some might argue that the purpose of fortification is to reach the severely deficient, or children, the elderly or pregnant mothers. Maybe! But again, if you could press a button and remove fortification from everyone else, would you feel comfortable pushing that button? Remember, trials don’t test going down from current levels, only going up.有人可能会认为，强化食品的目的是为了惠及严重缺乏营养的人群，或是儿童、老人、孕妇等群体。或许确实如此！但再想想，如果你能按下一个按钮，取消给其他所有人的食品强化，你会敢按下这个按钮吗？要知道，相关试验只测试了在现有水平基础上增加强化剂量，而非减少强化剂量。

## So that’s my story 这就是我的观点

- Biology and evolution suggest a prior that moderate levels of vitamin D (say 80 nmol/L) are quite possibly better than low levels (like 40 nmol/L) and unlikely to be worse.生物学和进化论表明，一个先验结论是，中等水平的维生素D（比如80纳摩尔/升）很可能比低水平（如40纳摩尔/升）更好，且不太可能更差。
- Observational studies say that vitamin D is magical, but those studies are bad and we should ignore them.观察性研究称维生素D具有神奇功效，但这些研究并不可靠，我们应当不予理会。
- The RCTs show that vitamin D is non-miraculous. But beyond that they don’t provide much information, because they mostly enrolled people with moderate vitamin D levels, meaning plausible effects would require colossal sample sizes to reliably detect.随机对照试验表明维生素D并非神奇之物。但除此之外，这些试验并未提供太多信息，因为它们招募的大多是维生素D水平中等的人群，这意味着若要可靠检测出其潜在效果，需要极大的样本量。
- What evidence the RCTs do provide points weakly towards a modest benefit.随机对照试验所提供的相关证据，也只是微弱地指向其具有一定的益处。
- If real, that benefit would far exceed the cost of taking vitamin D.即便这种益处真的存在，也会远超服用维生素D的成本。
- Therefore, if you have low vitamin D, it seems wise to supplement.因此，如果你体内的维生素D水平偏低，补充维生素D似乎是明智之举。

This is all very weak, I know! But sometimes weak evidence is all we’ve got.我知道这一切都缺乏有力依据！但有时候我们能掌握的就只有这些不充分的证据。

I wish we had at least *one* large trial done in a population with low starting levels. But as far as I can tell, none are underway. In fact, it’s [unlikely](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsbmb.2025.106895) that there will be any more large trials anytime soon. So weak evidence is how it’s going to be.我希望我们至少有一项 *针对* 低起始水平人群的大型试验已经完成。但据我所知，目前没有任何试验正在进行。事实上，短期内 [不太可能](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsbmb.2025.106895) 再开展任何新的大型试验。所以，目前只能依靠薄弱的证据。

If you change the hazard ratio by a factor of *HR*, then the new in life expectancy is *L(HR) = ∫ S(t)ᴴᴿ dt*, so the change under a linear approximation is *ΔL ≈ (HR-1) × L’(1)*.
