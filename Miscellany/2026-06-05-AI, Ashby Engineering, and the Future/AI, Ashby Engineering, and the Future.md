## AI, Ashby Engineering, and the Future人工智能、Ashby 工程与未来

More AI-written code. The sky didn’t fall.**自2025年8月以来，进入Ashby生产系统的新代码中有一半以上是AI生成的** ，但客户问题总体保持稳定。见下图。客户更多了。AI编写的代码也更多了。但天并没有塌下来。

*We have a blip in March / April every year; these cyclical patterns aren’t relevant to explain here. Cursor provides stats on how much of our code is generated by AI*.我们每年3月/4月都会出现一次短暂波动；这些周期性模式在此处无需解释。Cursor 会提供关于我们有多少代码由人工智能生成的统计数据。

We’ve also not seen any regressions in code quality, velocity, or onboarding time for engineers (anecdotally, we’ve seen comprehension of the codebase increase!).我们也没有看到工程师在代码质量、开发速度或入职时间方面出现任何倒退（据观察，我们发现大家对代码库的理解能力有所提升！）。

This isn’t a toy project. Ashby is a suite of talent acquisition software with over 100,000 weekly active users, millions of candidate applications per week, and features that resemble entire companies' worth of product (like Calendly and Looker). 这可不是一个玩具项目。Ashby 是一套人才招聘软件套件，每周活跃用户超过10万人，每周处理数百万份候选人申请，其功能堪比一整家公司的产品（比如 Calendly 和 Looker）。

I’m Colin, Head of EMEA Engineering at Ashby. I want to share with you how Ashby Engineering is thinking about AI and the changes it brings to how we work. I’m going to assume you’re an engineer.我是科林，阿什比公司欧洲、中东和非洲地区工程部门负责人。我想与大家分享阿什比工程部门对人工智能的看法，以及它给我们工作方式带来的变革。我将假设大家都是工程师。

**Our thesis is that the cost of producing code is heading towards zero**. AI isn’t coming for our jobs, it’s coming for the mechanical parts of them: syntax, glue code, and the tip-taps of **keystrokes**. The parts that are less interesting, less challenging.**我们的论点是，编写代码的成本正趋近于零** 。人工智能不是来取代我们的工作，而是来取代工作中机械的部分：语法、粘合代码，以及 **按键** 的敲击动作。这些都是不那么有趣、更具挑战性的部分。

The part that matters for engineers - your judgment, your taste, your understanding of our customers - is getting more important, not less. Your value as an engineer was always weighted in your judgment. Every efficiency gain in producing high-quality code shifted the role further in that direction. AI will be a larger shift than we’ve seen before.对工程师而言至关重要的部分——你的判断力、你的品味、你对客户的理解——正变得越来越重要，而非相反。作为工程师，你的价值向来由判断力来衡量。写出高质量代码时效率的每一次提升，都在进一步推动角色朝这个方向发展。人工智能将带来一场比以往任何变革都更为巨大的转变。

That shift is already here. “Almost all my PRs are entirely AI-written now. I implemented an entire data ingestion via AI… It's ~40 PRs” - Tom, one of our engineers.这种转变已经到来。“现在我几乎所有的代码合并请求（PR）都是完全由AI撰写的。我通过AI实现了一整套数据摄取功能……大概有40个代码合并请求（PR）”——我们的工程师汤姆这样说。

Like any emerging technology, the industry is figuring out how to use AI effectively to build software. When to trust it, when to override it, and what needs to change in our systems so that "move fast" doesn't become "move recklessly." It's a shared mental model, and I expect it to evolve as we learn.和任何新兴技术一样，行业正在探索如何有效运用人工智能来开发软件。何时信任它、何时推翻它的判断，以及我们的系统需要做出哪些调整，才能避免“快速推进”变成“鲁莽行事”。这是一种共通的思维模式，我相信随着我们不断学习，这种模式也会不断演变。

## The Ground Rules 基本准则

As we use LLMs more and the world around us shifts, we believe there are two ground rules:随着我们对大语言模型（LLMs）的使用日益增多，周围的世界也在不断变化，我们认为有两条基本原则：

- Empathy cannot be replaced by AI 共情能力无法被人工智能取代
- You are responsible for what you ship 你要为你交付的内容负责

### Empathy Cannot Be Replaced by AI 共情无法被人工智能取代

**Building products is a human endeavor**. LLMs do not have taste. They do not know our customers. They cannot feel or understand the frustration of using a bad product or the delight of using an exceptional one. That still requires judgment, and, in a world where building a functional product is insanely fast, the ability to build a great one is even more important.**打造产品是一项人类的事业** 。大语言模型没有审美，它们不了解我们的客户，也无法感知或理解使用劣质产品的挫败感，以及体验卓越产品的愉悦感。这一切仍需要判断力，而在打造一款功能性产品的速度已经快得惊人的当下，打造出优质产品的能力就显得更为关键了。

**We also value individual focus, so when we collaborate, it’s important we do it effectively**. We don’t do mindless standups. We don’t do planning poker. We do write documents for our colleagues to read and understand. We do ask for help with reviewing changes.**我们也重视个人专注，因此在协作时，高效合作至关重要** 。我们不搞无意义的每日站会，也不玩计划扑克。我们会撰写文档供同事阅读理解，也会请求他人协助审核修改内容。

Empathy means remembering to write these documents for the humans who will read them. LLMs can help with writing. But, without guidance, LLMs will write documents that seem convincing yet are hard for humans to read, full of unimportant details, and lacking joy and wisdom. Here’s an excerpt of a PR description I had an LLM write:同理心意味着要记得为那些会阅读这些文档的人来撰写它们。大语言模型可以辅助写作。但如果缺乏指导，大语言模型写出的文档看似有说服力，实则人类难以读懂，充斥着无关紧要的细节，且缺乏情感与智慧。以下是我让大语言模型撰写的一份产品发布说明节选：

```
Added .github/workflows/pr-relevant-test-coverage.yml:
  - Triggers on pull_request (excluding master) and
    workflow_dispatch with pr_number.
  - Resolves PR number, collects changed files, and asks
    Claude to output up to 15 relevant test files.
```

This is all information that we can trivially figure out from reading the PR, and the full description was close to 30 lines. The most useful line still missed the mark:这些都是我们阅读PR时能轻松弄清楚的所有信息，完整描述接近30行。而最有用的那一行还是没说到点子上：

```
Coverage is intentionally not full-suite coverage; it
reflects only Claude-selected relevant tests against
changed files.
```

Why? Why does this not run full-suite coverage? **This description does not respect our colleague’s time**. It is devoid of empathy for us as reviewers and future maintainers of this code.为什么？为什么不进行全量测试覆盖？ **这种描述没有尊重同事的时间** 。对于作为代码审阅者和未来维护者的我们来说，它毫无同理心。

```
Coverage is intentionally not full-suite coverage. The
full suite with coverage takes hours to run. We are
using this to give guidance to engineers on where risks
lie.
```

Remember what empowers our colleagues to help us. Don’t cede writing documents for humans to LLMs.记住是什么让我们的同事愿意帮助我们。不要把写给人类看的文档撰写工作交给大语言模型。

### You are responsible for what you ship你要为你交付的内容负责

LLMs can be wonderfully wrong. Confidently incorrect. Inexplicably careless. **The biggest risk with AI isn’t that it’s wrong. It’s that it sounds right**.大语言模型可能会错得离谱。自信地给出错误答案，又莫名其妙地粗心大意。 **人工智能最大的风险不在于它出错了，而在于它听起来言之有理** 。

*“I didn’t mean to remove the tar-stream package - it was an accidental casualty when I was editing backend/package.json…”* - Claude Code “我不是故意要删除 tar-stream 包的——这是我在编辑 backend/package.json 时不小心删掉的……”——Claude Code

**You are responsible for what you ship**. Whether every line is handwritten or an LLM generated the entire PR. You are responsible for understanding what the code does, why it does it, and what happens when it breaks.**你要为你交付的内容负责** 。无论每一行代码是手写的，还是由大模型生成了整个拉取请求。你有责任理解代码的功能、编写原因，以及代码出错时会发生什么。

As we use LLMs more, skepticism has to increase, not decrease. Ask for alternatives. Ask for edge cases. Ask it to critique itself. Understand the reasoning before accepting the output. 随着我们对大语言模型（LLMs）的使用越来越多，质疑态度必须不断增强，而非减弱。要寻求替代方案，关注边缘情况，让模型自我批判，并在接受输出前理解其推理过程。

### Think More, Think Harder 多思考，深思熟虑

**We must think more - and think harder than before**. LLMs make it easy to no-brain your way through something. Resist that urge. Stay vigilant. It is easy to throw an issue at an LLM, have the PR description auto-generated, get the LLM to write the tests, and throw the PR out for review… all whilst fixing the entirely wrong issue or building a subpar solution.**我们必须多思考——而且要比以往更深入地思考** 。大语言模型让你可以不假思索地应付各种事情。请抵制这种冲动。保持警惕。你很容易把一个问题丢给大语言模型，让它自动生成需求描述（PR）、编写测试，然后将需求合并请求（PR）提交审核……但与此同时，你解决的可能是完全错误的问题，或者做出的是一个不达标的解决方案。

A particularly nefarious manifestation of this is running lots of agents in parallel on disparate tasks. This is multi-tasking on steroids. Multitasking is ineffective because the human brain can’t focus on multiple high-level tasks simultaneously. It may feel super productive to have five agents working on five issues and flicking between them all, but are you really making your best decisions? Are you able to think deeply about the guidance each agent needs? Do you understand what is being built?这种做法的一个特别恶劣的表现是让大量智能体并行处理不同的任务。这是极致的多任务处理。多任务处理效率低下，因为人类大脑无法同时专注于多项高层次任务。让五个智能体处理五个问题，还在它们之间不停切换，可能会让人觉得效率极高，但你真的能做出最明智的决策吗？你能深入思考每个智能体所需的指导吗？你清楚正在构建的内容是什么吗？

The current hype cycle often emphasizes quantity and velocity above all else, while ignoring quality and ingenuity, or with the promise that somehow these outcomes will follow. At Ashby, we are not succumbing to this pressure. It’s a myopic view of the world where everything can and should be shortcutted. Shortcuts always existed, and many of them reduce the quality and ingenuity of our work:当下的炒作周期往往将数量和速度置于首位，却忽视质量与独创性，或是寄望于这些特质会自然而然地随之而来。在阿什比，我们不会屈从于这种压力。这种看待世界的视角目光短浅，认为万事皆可、也都应该走捷径。捷径向来存在，而其中许多捷径都会降低我们工作的质量与独创性：

![](https://a-us.storyblok.com/f/1017310/1474x922/c30c970abe/ezra-klein-case-against-writing.png)

![](https://a-us.storyblok.com/f/1017310/1438x532/9e63d83014/tao-distant.png)

These external quotes reflect our own journey to success. Before AI, we could have always moved faster: we could have outsourced work to contractors, we could have built features instead of building blocks, we could have launched earlier. But the hours we spent hiring quality folks instead of outsourcing, thinking of abstractions instead of coding, and being patient with our product were often more impactful than the alternative. Thinking deeply is part of why we’re a successful startup today, and we’re not stopping.这些外部评价印证了我们走向成功的历程。在人工智能出现之前，我们本可以一直走得更快：我们本可以将工作外包给承包商，本可以直接打造功能而非搭建基础模块，本可以更早推出产品。但我们花时间招聘优秀人才而非外包、专注构建抽象概念而非编写代码、对产品保持耐心，这些付出往往比另一种选择更有成效。深度思考是我们如今成为成功初创企业的原因之一，而且我们不会停下脚步。

### Specs are Still for Humans 需求说明仍需人工处理

One of the shifts we’re seeing is the intention to feed specs to LLMs.We've always valued specs. They derisk development and ensure alignment. LLMs also benefit from the context that specs can provide.我们看到的一个转变是，人们打算将技术规范输入给大语言模型。我们一直都很重视技术规范，它们能降低开发风险并确保一致性。大语言模型也能从技术规范所提供的上下文信息中获益。

However, **what a human needs from a spec and what an LLM needs from a spec are different**. As humans, we need something that is mindful of our time, engages us as readers, and focuses our attention on the decisions that matter. E.g., something that tells us why you’ve decided to use Redis instead of Postgres vs a document detailing every single possible value for a new enum.然而， **人类对规范的需求与大语言模型（LLM）对规范的需求是不同的** 。作为人类，我们需要的内容要兼顾我们的时间成本，能吸引我们作为读者阅读，并让我们将注意力集中在关键决策上。例如，我们需要的是能说明你为何选择用 Redis 而非 Postgres 的内容，而非详细罗列新枚举的所有可能取值的文档。

We need something empathetic to us.我们需要能共情我们的东西。

**We must continue writing specs for humans**. Specs are focused on the expensive-to-change decisions. Specs reduce the risk that we build the wrong thing. They identify the abstractions that we’re going to need. For example, I was talking with one of our engineers about a requirement to perform an action on potentially millions of forms, and our framework doesn’t support that. Do they create a one-off implementation for their use case or figure out how to improve the bulk action building block? That’s the kind of decision we need to capture. It completely changes the implementation. It affects everyone else. Get it right, and we’ve created leverage for the next person.**我们必须继续为人类编写规范** 。规范聚焦于那些修改成本高昂的决策。规范能降低我们做出错误构建的风险。它们明确了我们将需要的抽象概念。例如，我曾和一位工程师讨论过一项需求：要对数百万份潜在表单执行某项操作，而我们的框架并不支持这一点。他们是为自身的用例创建一次性实现方案，还是想办法改进批量操作构建块？这正是我们需要明确的决策。它会彻底改变实现方式，也会影响其他所有人。做出正确的决策，就能为后续的人创造便利条件。

**Specs are written for humans**. An LLM might consume them as useful additional context. **规范是为人类编写的** 。大语言模型可能会将其作为有用的额外参考信息。

## How to Think About LLMs 如何看待大语言模型

We’ve set the ground rules and discussed how we want to interact as humans. Now: how we think about operating with LLMs.我们已经制定了基本规则，也探讨了人类之间的互动方式。现在，我们来思考如何与大语言模型（LLMs）协作运行。

There’s a lot of great material out there that gives an introduction to how LLMs work (市面上有很多优质资料介绍大语言模型的工作原理（ [this post 这篇文章](https://ngrok.com/blog/prompt-caching) by Sam Rose is a good one). 由山姆·罗斯撰写的这篇文章就是个不错的选择）

First, LLMs are not lazy. They will produce code. And keep going. They won’t stop and ask, “Should I create an abstraction for this?” They’re great at summarizing swathes of information. They’re not novel thinkers. They won’t make the mental leaps that allow you to simplify something and delete thousands of lines of code.首先，大语言模型并不“懒惰”。它们会生成代码并持续推进，不会停下来问“我应该为这个创建抽象层吗？”它们擅长汇总大量信息，但并非富有创意的思考者，不会做出能让你简化问题、删除数千行代码的思维跳跃。

A simple model I use is to think of the LLM as a set of dice, not the hyped superintelligence. 我用一个简单的模型来将大语言模型（LLM）看作是一组骰子，而非被过度吹捧的超级智能。

Some problems LLMs are good at, and you don’t need to roll a high score for. They’re great at summarizing documents, finding patterns, and continuing them.大模型擅长解决一些问题，你不需要在这些问题上拿到高分。它们非常擅长总结文档、发现规律并续写内容。

Some problems they’re terrible at. You’ll never get enough sixes in a row. Counting the number of r's in “strawberry”, multiplying large numbers, or figuring out which direction you're facing after a series of turns. These feel like they should be easy, but they require a kind of precision that dice just can't reliably give you.有些事情他们根本不擅长。你永远不可能连续掷出足够多的六点。数“草莓”这个单词里字母r的个数、做大数乘法，或是在经过一系列转向后弄清楚自己面朝哪个方向。这些事看似简单，却需要一种骰子无法稳定提供的精准度。

And then there are ways to load the dice to tip the odds in your favor, like giving them an example of what good looks like.还有一些方法可以让你占得先机，比如给他们展示一个优秀范例。

With that model in mind, here's how working with LLMs plays out in practice.带着这个模型，我们来看看与大语言模型（LLMs）协作在实际中是如何进行的。

## Two Modes of Working With AI 与AI协作的两种模式

I see two distinct ways to work with LLMs: as a sidekick or as a delegate. Recognizing which mode you're in and which you should be in is the key skill.我发现与大语言模型（LLMs）协作有两种截然不同的方式：作为助手或作为代表。认清自己处于哪种模式以及应该处于哪种模式，是关键的技能。

**Default to sidekick mode**. You’re using AI to explore the codebase, find and digest large amounts of information, and implement the detailed specs you’ve written. **You** are making most of the decisions.**默认使用助手模式** 。你正借助人工智能探索代码库，查找并梳理大量信息，同时落实你编写的详细规范。 **你** 将做出绝大多数决策。

This is the mode for anything high-risk: database migrations, candidate data handling, security-sensitive code, and architectural decisions. These are the places where “looks right” isn’t good enough. You need to be in the driver's seat.这是适用于所有高风险场景的模式：数据库迁移、候选数据处理、安全敏感代码以及架构决策。在这些场景中，“看起来没问题”远远不够。你必须牢牢掌控全局。

**Switch to delegate mode when the blast radius is small**. You review the output - or sometimes you don't. Prototyping, local tools, and operations tools are great candidates here. You can move fast because the cost of failure is low.**当影响范围较小时，切换至委托模式** 。你只需审核输出结果——有时甚至无需审核。原型设计、本地工具和运维工具都非常适合这种模式。你可以快速推进，因为失败的成本很低。

Most engineers will over-delegate at first. Then they’ll over-correct and under-delegate. The question is never "should I use AI?" - it's "how much should I trust it here?" Think about what happens if the code is wrong. Is it embarrassing? Expensive? **Existential**?大多数工程师一开始都会过度授权。然后他们又会矫枉过正，授权不足。问题从来都不是“我是否应该使用人工智能？”——而是“在这里我应该信任它到什么程度？”想想如果代码出错会发生什么。是尴尬吗？代价高昂吗？ **关乎生死存亡** 吗？

**Blending between the two** will be common. That’s where your judgment matters, and where breaking up the task pays off. You might start off by having the LLM build the scaffolding of the new feature you’re working on, hand-write some SQL queries, and finally jump back to full delegation for writing a few unit tests.两者的融合</b>会很常见。这正是你的判断力发挥作用的地方，也是拆分任务能带来回报的地方。你可以先让大模型搭建你正在开发的新功能的框架，手动编写一些 SQL 查询，最后再完全交由它来编写几个单元测试。

## How We’re Using AI Today 我们如今如何使用人工智能

We’re actively encouraging engineers to use AI tools to support their work. We’re doing this through education, workshops, pairing, and generous token budgets. **We do not mandate the use of AI or measure token usage**. Our belief is that doing so encourages slop.我们积极鼓励工程师使用 AI 工具辅助工作，具体通过培训、研讨会、结对协作以及充足的令牌额度来实现这一目标。 **我们不会强制要求使用 AI，也不会统计令牌使用量** 。我们认为这样做会滋生敷衍了事的态度。

As writing code gets cheaper, safety becomes more important. **Safety has to be built into the infrastructure, not imposed as discipline**. We think of safety using a Swiss Cheese model. Every layer has holes, but the holes are in different places. Tests catch what review misses, feature flags catch what tests miss, and observability catches what slipped past everything else.随着编写代码的成本越来越低，安全性就变得愈发重要。 **安全必须融入基础设施，而非作为约束强加** 。我们采用“瑞士奶酪模型”来考量安全性。每一层都存在漏洞，但漏洞的位置各不相同。测试能发现代码审查遗漏的问题，功能标志能发现测试遗漏的问题，可观测性则能发现逃过所有环节的问题。

**We start by giving all our engineers access to AI code generation tools**. ### Change Review 变更审核

At Ashby, we already review code changes. Our reviews are fairly broad and cover everything from bug spotting and abstraction opportunities to code documentation. The coming increases in output will outpace our ability to review. We need to be critical of why we review code. We need to make sure we’re spending our review time on the highest value targets.在阿什比公司，我们已经在开展代码审查工作。我们的审查范围相当广泛，涵盖了漏洞排查、抽象优化机会以及代码文档等方方面面。未来产出量的增长速度将超过我们的审查能力。我们必须明确代码审查的核心目的，确保将审查时间投入到价值最高的工作中。

Humans are bad at catching bugs. Engineers shouldn't spend time thinking about whether someone else has used useMemo correctly in a component. We aren't here to be human linters. Humans shouldn’t spend significant time reviewing each line of another human’s code. Self-review and other tooling (e.g., linting) should handle 90% of that. If you’re reviewing, for example, an automated refactor to extract a function in 2026, we’ve failed.人类并不擅长抓出代码漏洞。工程师不该花时间去思考别人是否在组件中正确使用了 useMemo。我们不是来做人工代码检查的。人类也不应该花费大量时间去逐行审阅他人的代码。自我审查和其他工具（比如代码检查工具）应该能解决其中90%的问题。举例来说，如果你审核的是2026年为提取某个函数而进行的自动化重构，那说明我们之前的工作已经失败了。

So what are we here to review? 那么我们要审核什么呢？

- Does the **change** make sense? 这个 **变更** 合理吗？
- What are the high-risk areas? How can we reduce those risks?哪些是高风险领域？我们该如何降低这些风险？
- What are the performance characteristics?其性能特征是什么？
- Are the abstractions sensible? Are we abstracting enough?这些抽象是否合理？我们的抽象程度是否足够？

That last point on sensible abstractions deserves emphasis. LLMs are biased toward generating new code rather than reusing what exists. Left unchecked, they'll build you a codebase that works but that no one can navigate. LLMs don’t care if the code they’ve produced is complicated. Pushing toward simplicity is now one of the most valuable things a reviewer can do. This happened to me on a side project - there were two “interface modes,” and the LLM had created copies of everything in each mode! I only realized when I fixed the same bug twice.关于合理抽象的最后一点值得强调。大语言模型倾向于生成新代码，而非复用现有内容。若不加干预，它们会为你构建出一个能运行但无人能理清的代码库。大语言模型并不在意自己生成的代码是否复杂。如今，推动代码简化是代码评审者能做的最有价值的事之一。我在一个个人副业项目中就遇到过这种情况——存在两种“界面模式”，而大语言模型为每种模式都复制了所有内容！我是在两次修复同一个 bug 时才发现这个问题的。

We’re continuing to invest in this area. There are many interesting avenues to explore and experiment with. Can an LLM give a first-pass design critique for new UIs? Can they do a valuable critique of a spec? 我们将继续在这一领域进行投入。有许多有趣的方向值得探索和尝试。大语言模型能否为新的用户界面提供初步的设计点评？它们能否对产品规范做出有价值的点评？
