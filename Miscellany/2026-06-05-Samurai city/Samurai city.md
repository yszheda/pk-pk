## For three hundred years, Japan enjoyed enviable stability and peace. All it took was locking up its warlike samurai elite in the world’s least efficient city. 三百年来，日本享有令人艳羡的稳定与和平。而这一切，不过是将好战的武士精英困在这座世上效率最低的城市里罢了。

Cities are often centers of agglomeration, places where people gather to collaborate with one another. But this is not the only reason they exist. Sometimes, cities are chiefly centers of consumption, where elites gather to devour resources extracted from the rest of the country. And occasionally, they are something like prisons, where troublesome social groups are concentrated so that the authorities can keep an eye on them. Many premodern cities, like Versailles, Naples, or Imperial Rome, were a little like this. But perhaps the greatest example was Tokugawa Edo.城市往往是集聚中心，是人们聚集起来相互协作的地方。但这并非城市存在的唯一原因。有时，城市主要是消费中心，精英阶层聚集于此，消耗从全国其他地方掠夺来的资源。偶尔，城市还会沦为类似监狱的存在，将麻烦的社会群体集中在此，以便当局对他们进行监视。许多前现代城市，如凡尔赛宫、那不勒斯或古罗马帝国的都城，都多少是这般模样。而德川时代的江户，或许是其中最典型的例子。

Between 1600 and 1868, Japan was dominated by the Tokugawa family. The Tokugawas had prevailed over their rivals after a series of civil wars, establishing a sort of dictatorship known as the Shogunate. They developed a remarkable social system, crafted to preserve their power, and with it, the peace and social stability of Japan. At the apex of this system was the city of Edo (today’s Tokyo), at times the largest city in the world, and one of the strangest urban structures in history.1600年至1868年间，日本由德川家族统治。德川氏在一系列内战中击败对手，建立了被称为幕府的独裁统治形式。他们构建了一套精妙的社会制度，旨在维护自身权力，同时也维系着日本的和平与社会稳定。这一体系的核心是江户城（今东京），它曾是世界上最大的城市之一，也是历史上最奇特的城市格局之一。

### The Tokugawa social system 德川幕府社会制度

In early modern Europe, most people were tenant farmers, who paid rent to landowners. The state sometimes taxed landowners, sometimes tenants, and sometimes both through consumption taxes. In peacetime, however, the early modern state did not do very much, so taxes generally ran at just a few percent of national income.在近代早期的欧洲，大多数人都是佃农，他们需要向土地所有者缴纳租金。国家有时会向土地所有者征税，有时向佃农征税，有时也会通过消费税对两者同时征税。不过，在和平时期，近代早期的国家职能有限，因此税收通常只占国民收入的百分之几。

The picture in Japan was profoundly different. The peasantry was directly taxed by the government, at rates varying from [15 to 70 percent](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/137080/1/Binder3.pdf) of the harvest, with 40 percent as a rough norm. The authorities distributed most of their tax receipts to the samurai, a hereditary, quasi-noble class making up about six percent of the population. In both cases, the agricultural surplus ended up in the hands of a leisure class, but the Japanese system was structured very differently, with the surplus reaching the leisure class only through the funnel of public taxation.日本的情况则截然不同。农民直接向政府缴税，税率在收成的 [15%至70%](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/137080/1/Binder3.pdf) 之间浮动，40%是大致的标准税率。当局将大部分税收收入发放给武士阶层——这是一个世袭的准贵族阶级，约占总人口的6%。在这两种情况下，农业剩余最终都落入了有闲阶级手中，但日本的制度结构截然不同，剩余仅通过公共税收这一渠道流向有闲阶级。

![Adapted from a map by Fabian Drixler, based on a 2015 exhibit at Yale's Peabody Museum.](https://worksinprogress.co/.netlify/images?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassets.worksinprogress.co%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F06%2Fgildedprison-map.png&w=2000&h=2415&fit=cover)

Only a minority of Japan was directly ruled by the Shogunate. Most of the country was administered by various classes of daimyo: shinpan (collateral branches of the Tokugawa family), fudai (longstanding Tokugawa vassals) and tozama (formerly independent daimyo families who had submitted after the Tokugawa victory in the civil wars). 日本只有少数地区由幕府直接统治。该国大部分地区由不同阶层的大名管理：亲藩（德川氏的旁支）、谱代（长期效忠于德川的家臣）以及外样（在内战中德川氏获胜后才归顺的原独立大名家族）。 Image 图片 Adapted from a map by Fabian Drixler, based on a 2015 exhibit at Yale's Peabody Museum. 改编自法比安·德里克斯勒绘制的地图，该地图基于2015年耶鲁大学皮博迪博物馆的展览。

Measured by agricultural output, [about 15 percent of Japan](https://www.bakumatsu.ru/lib/Making_of_modern_Japan_Marius_B._Jansen.pdf) fell under the direct control of the Shogunate. These were areas that had always belonged to the Tokugawa, or that had been conquered by them during the civil wars. About a tenth of samurai were Tokugawa retainers, who received their stipends directly from the Shogunate.从农业产出来看， [日本约15%的土地](https://www.bakumatsu.ru/lib/Making_of_modern_Japan_Marius_B._Jansen.pdf) 处于幕府的直接管辖之下。这些区域要么原本就属于德川氏，要么是他们在内战期间征服所得。大约十分之一的武士是德川氏的家臣，他们的俸禄直接由幕府发放。

In about three quarters of Japan, the tax authorities were a different group, the daimyo. The daimyo had originally been the territorial nobility, and in the pre-Tokugawa era, the breakdown of central authority had left them as the effective rulers of their domains. After 1600 the surviving daimyo submitted to the Tokugawa and were rewarded with a role somewhat akin to that of regional governors. There were about 260 daimyo, and about nine tenths of samurai were their retainers.在日本约四分之三的地区，税务部门是另一类群体——大名。大名原本是地方贵族，在德川幕府建立之前，中央权力瓦解，他们成为了各自领地的实际统治者。1600年之后，幸存的大名臣服于德川幕府，并获得了类似地方长官的职位。当时约有260位大名，而武士中约十分之九都是他们的家臣。

### Urban design as a panopticon for the nobility贵族的全景式城市设计

The city of Edo played a crucial role in this system. The daimyo were required to maintain mansions in Edo, in which their families were obliged to live permanently. Any act of disloyalty by a daimyo would thus place his family in the gravest peril. Most women of the daimyo class passed their lives as effective hostages of the state, never visiting the domains that their husbands, fathers, and sons governed. The daimyo themselves were also required to spend alternate years or half-years in Edo, bringing with them great crowds of samurai retainers. Edo thus had a dual nature: on the one hand, it was the apex of Japanese society, in which the country’s agricultural surplus was consumed; on the other, it was a kind of prison, in which Japan’s potentially dangerous elites were contained and monitored.江户城在这一体系中扮演了至关重要的角色。大名们被要求在江户修建宅邸，其家眷必须永久居住于此。因此，大名若有任何不忠之举，其家人都将面临灭顶之灾。大多数大名阶层的女性一生都如同国家的有效人质，从未踏足丈夫、父亲和儿子所治理的领地。大名本人也被要求每隔一年或半年前往江户居住，并随行大批武士家臣。江户因此具有双重属性：一方面，它是日本社会的顶峰，全国的农业剩余产品在此消耗；另一方面，它又像一座监狱，将日本那些潜在的危险精英阶层控制并监视起来。

Edo was zoned for social class centuries before the development of zoning in the West. Samurai and daimyo districts were concentrated on the higher ground to the west, while commoners were crowded between Edo Castle and the Sumida River. 早在西方 分区规划体系形成 数百年前，江户就已按社会阶层进行分区。武士和大名的居住区集中在西部的高地上，而普通百姓则挤在江户城与隅田川之间。 Image 图片 André Sorensen, The Making of Urban Japan, Cities and Planning from Edo to the Twenty-First Century. Sorensen's map was adapted from another drawn by Okata Junishiro in 1981, itself based on Tokugawa originals. 安德烈·索伦森：《日本城市的形成：从江户到21世纪的城市与规划》。索伦森的地图改编自冈田顺四郎1981年绘制的另一幅地图，而冈田顺四郎的地图又以德川时代的原版地图为基础。

This gave Edo a peculiar demographic and economic character. For one thing, it was enormous. Japanese rice agriculture was extremely productive by premodern standards and generated a huge surplus for the capital’s benefit. Edo’s population seems to have been [over a million by 1700](https://www.bakumatsu.ru/lib/Making_of_modern_Japan_Marius_B._Jansen.pdf), which would make it the largest city in the world at the time: London reached a population of a million only around 1800, while New York did not reach it until 1880. 这赋予了江户独特的人口与经济特征。一方面，它规模庞大。以近代以前的标准来看，日本的水稻农业极具生产力，为首都带来了巨额盈余。江户的人口在1700年时似乎已 [超过百万](https://www.bakumatsu.ru/lib/Making_of_modern_Japan_Marius_B._Jansen.pdf) ，这使其成为当时世界上最大的城市：伦敦直到19世纪左右才达到百万人口，而纽约则到1880年才实现这一目标。

It was also extremely top-heavy socially. Unlike the knights of the European feudal system, Tokugawa-era samurai were usually required to live in cities. The Shogun’s own samurai were permanently concentrated in Edo, while each daimyo brought hundreds or thousands of his samurai with him during his years of residence in the capital. This meant that almost *half* of Edo’s population at any given time were samurai.这一阶层在社会结构中也呈现出极度头重脚轻的特点。与欧洲封建制度下的骑士不同，德川时代的武士通常被要求居住在城市。幕府自身的武士长期集中在江户，而每位大名在都城居住期间，都会带上数百乃至数千名武士。这意味着在任何时期，江户几乎 *一半* 的人口都是武士。

![Wikimedia Commons.](https://worksinprogress.co/.netlify/images?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassets.worksinprogress.co%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F06%2Fimage-4.png&w=900&h=1133&fit=cover)

Samurai, photographed in around 1880. While these men were dressed for the camera rather than for battle, samurai in fact made little use of the new military technologies developed after the pacification of Japan by the Tokugawa. In some ways, the Japanese army regressed technologically in the peaceful Tokugawa period, with gunpowder weapons displaced by less effective but more high-status equipment. 约1880年拍摄的武士。这些人虽是为拍照而非作战而着装，但实际上，在德川幕府平定日本之后，武士们几乎很少使用新研发的军事技术。在某些方面，日本军队在和平的德川幕府时期出现了技术倒退，火药武器被效力稍差但地位更高的装备所取代。 Image 图片 Wikimedia Commons. 维基共享资源。

The samurai were theoretically a warrior class, but since Tokugawa Japan was at peace, there was little real soldiering to do. Most non-military work was seen as degrading, with the exception of gentlemanly occupations like civil administration and tutoring in calligraphy. Most samurai thus lived as pensioners of the state, deriving the bulk of their income from hereditary government stipends assigned to their families at the start of the Tokugawa era. 理论上，武士是一个武士阶层，但由于德川日本处于和平时期，实际的作战任务寥寥无几。除了文官治理、书法教习这类体面职业外，大多数非军事工作都被视为有失身份。因此，大多数武士以国家养老金领取者的身份生活，其大部分收入来自德川幕府初期分配给家族的世袭政府俸禄。

Stipends were generous for high-ranking samurai but very modest for the majority: in 1876 the [Japanese state calculated](https://www-jstor-org.lonlib.idm.oclc.org/stable/43697706?seq=7) that only 5.1 percent of samurai had stipends worth more than 100 yen, equivalent to a little over $4,000 today. The result was that most samurai lived in dignified but extreme poverty. It is a testament to the primacy of status among human ends that, although some samurai surreptitiously took on side jobs to supplement their tiny incomes, very few abjured their rank to take up more gainful occupations openly. Interclass marriages were forbidden, and there was theoretically no process for ennoblement, though some wealthy commoners worked around this by arranging to be adopted into impecunious samurai families.高级武士的俸禄十分优厚，但对于大多数武士来说却相当微薄：1876年， [日本政府统计](https://www-jstor-org.lonlib.idm.oclc.org/stable/43697706?seq=7) 显示，只有5.1%的武士俸禄超过100日元，相当于如今略超4000美元。这导致大多数武士虽保有体面，却深陷极度贫困。这一点印证了身份在人类追求中的首要地位：尽管有些武士偷偷从事副业以补贴微薄收入，但极少有人放弃身份，公开从事利润更高的职业。阶级间的婚姻被禁止，理论上也没有晋升贵族的途径，不过一些富裕的平民通过安排过继给家境贫寒的武士家庭，绕开了这一规定。

### The High City 上城

Edo was zoned according to social class, probably the largest-scale use of zoning before modern times. Samurai neighborhoods made up a large minority of the total surface area, concentrated in western areas that came to be known as the High City. Higher-ranking samurai had substantial homes, but most lived in extremely austere conditions: the median family lived in a tiny, two-room rowhouse with no garden and communal latrines.江户按社会等级划分区域，这或许是近代之前规模最大的区域规划应用。武士居住区占总面积的相当一部分，集中在后来被称为“上城”的西部区域。高级武士拥有宽敞的宅邸，但大多数武士的生活条件极为简陋：普通家庭大多住在狭小的两排联排小屋内，没有庭院，还需共用公共厕所。

The samurai shared the High City with the daimyo. Each daimyo was expected to maintain several estates in Edo, including a more compact estate near the Shogun’s castle and a more spacious garden estate further out. These were made up of single-storey buildings connected by covered walkways, set amid exquisitely landscaped gardens. Each estate was surrounded by a perimeter wall broken by just one or two richly ornamented gates. The great expense of all this was, from the Shogunate’s perspective, part of its appeal: the more resources daimyo expended on maintaining mansions and circulating between them, the less remained for fomenting trouble. 武士与大名共同居住在高城。每位大名都需在江户拥有多处宅邸，包括幕府将军城堡附近规模较小的宅邸，以及郊外更为宽敞的园林宅邸。这些宅邸均为单层建筑，由带顶的回廊相连，坐落于精心设计的园林之中。每处宅邸都被围墙环绕，仅设有一两座装饰华丽的大门。从幕府的角度来看，维持这一切的巨额开销正是其用意所在：大名在修缮宅邸与往返宅邸之间耗费的资源越多，用于煽动叛乱的资源就越少。

The inner High City of Edo, photographed in 1865 or 1866. Walled daimyo compounds present austere facades to the public street. Most daimyo would also have had more spacious mansions in suburban areas of the High City, with extensive gardens. 1865年或1866年拍摄的江户内城。大名的城郭宅邸对着公共街道呈现出肃穆的外观。大多数大名在江户内城的郊区还拥有更宽敞的府邸，配有规模宏大的园林。 Image 图片 Wikimedia Commons. 维基共享资源 。

These samurai and daimyo neighborhoods made up a substantial majority of Edo’s surface area. In some ways, they foreshadowed affluent American suburbs, though they would have felt quite different at street level. Physically, the closest modern parallel might be elite neighborhoods in countries like South Africa or Brazil, where fear of crime has driven the wealthy behind high walls and electric fences. 这些武士和大名聚居区占据了江户绝大部分的面积。在某些方面，它们预示了富裕的美国郊区，不过在街道层面上给人的感觉会截然不同。从实体形态来看，最接近的现代类似区域或许是南非或巴西等国的精英社区，在这些地方，对犯罪的恐惧将富人赶到了高墙和电网之后。

In fact, however, Edo seems to have been an unusually safe city, a fact that [often struck](https://archive.org/stream/completejournalo00harr/completejournalo00harr_djvu.txt?) early Western visitors. Most people [had no locks](https://ia802806.us.archive.org/4/items/japandaybyday18711917mors/japandaybyday18711917mors.pdf?) on their front doors. The [English writer Isabella Bird](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2184/2184-h/2184-h.htm) wrote that a solitary foreign woman could travel in Japan in ‘perfect safety’ and that she had never encountered ‘a single instance of incivility’, though at home she would often have been ‘exposed to rudeness, insult, and extortion, if not to actual danger’. The walls and gates of the High City may thus tell us more about elite culture and status display than they do about any distinctive security needs.然而事实上，江户似乎是一座异常安全的城市，这一事实常常让早期的西方访客感到惊叹。大多数人的前门都没有上锁。英国作家伊莎贝拉·伯德写道，一位独居的外国女性可以“绝对安全地”在日本旅行，而且她从未遇到过“一次无礼之举”，而在国内，她即便没有遭遇实际危险，也常常会“遭受粗鲁对待、侮辱和勒索”。因此，上城的城墙和城门所反映的，或许更多是精英阶层的文化与身份彰显，而非其独特的安全需求。

### The Low City 下城

Much of the rest of Edo’s surface area was taken up with religious complexes and institutional buildings belonging to the Shogunate. The commoners were crowded into the remainder, mostly in riverside areas known as the Low City. Though most commoners were very poor, some were richer than most samurai, and even richer than minor daimyo. Some of the greatest artistic legacies of Tokugawa Japan, like ukiyo-e prints and the kabuki theater, arose from the patronage of this class. However wealthy they might become, however, commoners’ status was axiomatically lower than that of the samurai.江户其余大部分表面积被幕府的宗教建筑群和行政建筑占据。平民被挤在剩下的区域，大多位于被称为“下城”的沿河地带。尽管大多数平民极为贫困，但部分平民的财富超过了大多数武士，甚至比小大名还要富有。浮世绘版画、歌舞伎剧院等德川时代最伟大的一些艺术遗产，都源于这一阶层的资助。然而，无论平民变得多么富有，其地位在本质上都低于武士。

Ukiyo-e pictures were mass-produced using woodblock printing and sold widely among the urban middle classes. They were later to have a profound influence on Western visual arts, from Degas through Van Gogh to Hergé’s Tintin comics. 1863年，浮世绘艺术家广重描绘了一位大名及其随从队伍穿过上城的场景。大名在日本各地出行时会带着数百甚至数千名家臣，他们常常排成单列或双列行进在日本狭窄的道路上，队伍绵延可达数公里。注意看大名宅邸坚硬的外墙之后可见的庭院，以及队伍经过时俯身跪拜的平民。浮世绘作品通过木刻版画技术批量生产，在城市中产阶级中广泛流传。这些作品后来对西方视觉艺术产生了深远影响，从德加到梵高，再到埃尔热的《丁丁历险记》漫画，都能看到其影子。 Image 图片 Wikimedia Commons. 维基共享资源 。

Edo’s commoners worked mostly in providing goods and services to the city’s elite. The city as a whole produced little, consuming resources from the rest of Japan and providing little in return save the arguable gift of government. No international trade took place in Edo: the government was intensely wary of destabilizing foreign influences, and by the late-eighteenth century it allowed only two Dutch and [ten Chinese ships](http://camphor/) to visit Japan each year, all through the carefully controlled port at Nagasaki. (For comparison, about [fifteen thousand ships](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/11449/1/Robert_Keith_Sutcliffe_2013.pdf) arrived in British harbors from foreign ports annually in the same period.) In this as in other respects, the Shogunate’s political needs had a decidedly fitful relationship to the country’s economic growth: although the political stability that the regime provided certainly did foster prosperity, the steps it took to ensure that stability often stifled it.江户的平民大多为城中的精英阶层提供商品和服务。整座城市几乎不进行生产，消耗着日本其他地区的资源，除了政府这一颇具争议的“馈赠”外，几乎没有任何回报。江户没有开展任何国际贸易：政府极度警惕外来影响带来的不稳定，到18世纪末，仅允许每年有两艘荷兰船只和十艘中国船只到访日本，且所有船只都必须经由长崎港严格管控的航道停靠。（相比之下，同期每年约有一万五千艘船只从外国港口驶入英国港口。）在这方面以及其他诸多方面，幕府的政治需求与国家的经济发展之间存在着明显的不稳定关联：尽管该政权所提供的政治稳定无疑促进了繁荣，但其为保障这种稳定所采取的措施却常常抑制了经济发展。

![](https://worksinprogress.co/.netlify/images?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassets.worksinprogress.co%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F06%2Fgildedprison-diagram.png&w=2000&h=2517&fit=cover)

The physical form of the Low City was even more remarkable than that of the daimyo neighborhoods. Commoner Edo was divided into some 1,500-2,000 fenced and gated blocks called *chō*. These were then subdividedinto *roji*, alleys lined with small houses, often also gated. The Low City was thus divided up by many thousands of internal checkpoints, all of which closed at night. Edo was not under threat of attack in the Tokugawa period and the city as a whole was not fortified. The purpose of this immense labyrinth of walls and gates was to control and monitor the movement of the population.下城的实体形态甚至比大名街区更为引人注目。平民江户被划分为约1500至2000个设有围栏和大门的街区，称为 *町* 。这些街区又被细分为名为 *路地* 的小巷，小巷两侧排列着小房屋，通常也设有大门。因此，下城被数千个内部关卡分割开来，所有关卡都在夜间关闭。江户时代的江户并未面临攻击威胁，整座城市也未进行防御工事建设。这种由墙壁和大门构成的庞大迷宫，其目的在于管控和监督人口的流动。

Wealthy merchants lived in substantial houses called [*machiya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machiya), featuring a series of small garden courtyards around which living spaces were ingeniously laid out. In plan, *machiya* recall the [*domus* house type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus) of the urban middle and upper classes in the Roman Empire. Many *machiya* must have been rather agreeable houses, and some Japanese people still live in modernized *machiya* today. Most commoners, however, lived in [*nagaya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaya_\(architecture\)), tiny terraced houses frequently built back-to-back. A standard specification for *nagaya* was 2.7 meters wide and 3.6 meters deep, yielding a total floorspace of only 13.2 square meters. 富裕的商人住在被称为 [*町屋*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machiya) 的宽敞宅邸中，这类房屋设有一系列小型花园庭院，居住空间围绕庭院巧妙布局。从平面布局来看， *町屋* 让人联想到罗马帝国城市中上层阶级的 [*多姆斯式住宅*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus) 。许多 *町屋* 想必是相当舒适的住宅，如今仍有一些日本人居住在现代化改造后的 *町屋* 里。然而，大多数普通百姓住在 [*长屋*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaya_\(architecture\)) 中，这种狭窄的联排房屋常常背靠背建造。 *长屋* 的标准规格为宽2.7米、深3.6米，总建筑面积仅为13.2平方米。

An enormous number of people were crammed into commoner neighborhoods. The most crowded had [58,000 inhabitants per square kilometer](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780203993927/making-urban-japan-andr%C3%A9-sorensen), about twice the population density of [Manhattan today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Manhattan). Remarkably enough, however, their dwellings were largely single-storey. This seems to have been required by regulation, in turn motivated by fire risk or by dislike of commoner buildings towering over the mansions of the nobility. Japanese builders were perfectly capable of erecting taller structures, so the fact that these intensely crowded neighborhoods forewent the floorspace that additional storeys could have provided is not least among the paradoxes that Tokugawa Edo presents to history.大量民众挤在平民街区。最拥挤的区域每平方公里有 [5.8万居民](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780203993927/making-urban-japan-andr%C3%A9-sorensen) ，约为如今 [曼哈顿](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Manhattan) 人口密度的两倍。然而值得注意的是，他们的住所大多是单层建筑。这似乎是法规所要求的，而法规的制定则源于火灾风险的考量，或是出于不希望平民建筑高过贵族宅邸的意图。日本的建筑工匠完全有能力建造更高的建筑，因此这些极度拥挤的街区放弃了增加楼层所能提供的建筑面积，这一点正是德川时代的江户留给历史的诸多悖论之一。

---

In a way, the Tokugawa system was a success. Japan experienced near-total peace between 1600 and the late nineteenth century, a remarkable achievement for a premodern society and a dramatic contrast to Europe or China, where tens of millions of people died in wars. The Tokugawa succeeded in bribing, blackmailing, and intimidating Japan’s armed elites into a quarter of a millennium of passivity, enabling steady economic growth and a remarkable artistic flowering. One might thus argue that Edo’s apparent parasitism was an illusion. Edo was indeed a gilded prison, but prisons can be useful things.从某种程度上来说，德川幕府的制度是成功的。1600年至19世纪末，日本实现了近乎全面的和平，这对于一个前近代社会而言是一项非凡的成就，与欧洲或中国形成了鲜明对比——后两者有数千万人死于战乱。德川幕府通过贿赂、威逼和恐吓，让日本的武装精英阶层在长达250年的时间里保持顺从，这使得日本经济得以稳步发展，艺术领域也迎来了璀璨的繁荣。因此，有人或许会认为，江户表面上的寄生状态不过是一种假象。江户固然是一座镀金牢笼，但牢笼也并非毫无用处。

Still, it would have been nice if Japan had not needed a vast prison capital in the first place. Tokugawa Edo stands as a monument to the power of rent-seekers, producing little and demanding immense resources as a condition of civil peace. It shows how the physical form of cities may be reshaped by these demands, as governments apportion space and limit movement in line with their political needs. These forces yielded a city with tight restrictions on built density and land use, an acute housing shortage, and a huge share of privileged state stipendiaries. Edo is a particularly striking example of these phenomena. But it is far from alone.不过，要是日本从一开始就不需要庞大的监狱资本，那就好了。德川时代的江户是寻租者权力的见证，它几乎不创造任何东西，却以民事和平为条件索取大量资源。这表明，随着政府根据政治需求分配空间、限制人口流动，城市的物理形态可能会因这些需求而重塑。这些力量造就了一座建筑密度和土地使用受到严格限制、住房严重短缺、享有国家俸禄特权者占比极高的城市。江户是这些现象的典型例证，但绝非个例。
